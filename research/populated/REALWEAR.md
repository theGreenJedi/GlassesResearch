## Architecture and lifecycle
HMT-1/HMT-1Z1 established the rugged monocular Android model with 854×480 display and voice-first operation. Z1's intrinsically safe construction is a hardware advantage in hazardous environments but a wearability cost on the catalog-wide ruler. Both are now end-of-support; firmware ended at 12.6. HMT-1 nevertheless reached Android 10 and supports local dictation in several languages, showing that core operation was not wholly cloud-bound.

Navigator 500 adds modular camera and swappable battery. Navigator 520 and Z1 move to 1280×720 HD displays; Z1 adds intrinsically safe construction. RealWear states 500/520/Z1 remain supported through at least December 2030 and has planned Android-16-based software for currently supported devices in 2026.

## Ownership interpretation
Android enterprise application deployment gives meaningful owner/application control and practical local operation. The platform remains proprietary, so public app deployment should not be confused with open firmware, schematics or unrestricted low-level access.

## Evidence gaps
Current pricing/value, exact SDK/API exposure by generation, account/MDM dependence, repairability, battery/parts availability, prescription/PPE fit, firmware access and post-2030 support survival.
