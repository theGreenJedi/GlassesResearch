# Envision — populated research record

Source basis: `docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_ENVISION.md` (2026-08-12) and its cited Envision primary sources.

## Lineage
Two distinct assistive-eyewear branches: original Envision Glasses on Google Glass Enterprise Edition 2 hardware, and Ally Solos Glasses on Solos hardware. Read/Home/Professional are software/support editions, not hardware generations.

## Envision Glasses
Documented Glass EE2 hardware: Snapdragon XR1, 3 GB RAM, 32 GB storage, 640×360 monocular display, 8 MP camera, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth, AOSP 8.1 and ~46 g body weight. Envision software adds OCR, scene description, object/person finding, cash/color/light recognition, calling and conversational assistance.

Offline capability is meaningful: the lineage packet documents local text reading/scanning, cash recognition after currency download, light/color detection, QR scanning, object finding, people detection and Explore. Online operation improves accuracy and enables service-backed functions. This is partial cloud independence, not cloud-free operation.

## Ally Solos Glasses
Separate phone-connected branch using Solos hardware and the Ally app. Voice-first AI reads text, describes surroundings, translates, identifies objects, searches the web and accesses schedule/weather information; it can also act as headphones. Existing research documents up to 15 hours battery life with swappable battery stems and a $699 store price at the time of review.

## Ownership interpretation
Envision Glasses retain substantial offline assistive utility despite online enhancement. Ally Solos gain wearability and price advantages but depend more heavily on phone/app/AI services. Neither branch should be treated as an open developer platform merely because underlying hardware comes from Glass/Solos ecosystems.

## Evidence gaps
Prescription serviceability, repairability, long-term battery aging, exact account requirements, endpoint/model replacement, and post-vendor survival require deeper field-level evidence.
