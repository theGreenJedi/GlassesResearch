# State of Smart Glasses

Smart glasses are not one product category. Treating every head-worn computer as a direct competitor obscures the engineering tradeoffs that determine what a device can actually do.

## Functional families

### Camera + audio AI glasses
Conventional-looking frames centered on cameras, microphones, speakers and a phone/cloud assistant. Their strength is social wearability and capture; their central limitation is usually the absence of a visual display.

### HUD glasses
Frames with a small monocular or binocular information display. They can surface text, prompts, navigation or notifications without attempting full spatial computing. Evaluate field of view, brightness, eyebox and display placement separately from AI capability.

### Display viewers
Glasses whose primary job is to present a large virtual screen from a phone, computer or dedicated compute puck. These may be excellent displays while providing little environmental understanding.

### Spatial / AR systems
Devices combining displays with environmental sensing and tracking. Weight, power, thermals and visual architecture become substantially harder problems than in audio-camera glasses.

### Enterprise and task-specific systems
Industrial, medical, logistics and remote-assistance devices should be judged against the task they were designed to perform, not simply against consumer fashion glasses.

### Assistive glasses
Products designed around vision, hearing, navigation, captioning or cognitive assistance. Their value depends heavily on reliability, latency, accessibility design and service continuity.

### Open and developer-first platforms
Devices whose principal value is owner/developer access to sensors, display, firmware, APIs or compute pathways. These can score poorly as polished consumer products while remaining exceptionally important research platforms.

## Convergence to watch

The important long-term question is not which single form wins. It is which capabilities can coexist at ordinary-eyeglasses weight and power: all-day audio, useful cameras, private visual output, contextual sensing, local compute, user-selected AI, prescription lenses and meaningful owner control.

GlassesResearch tracks those capabilities independently so convergence can be measured rather than assumed from marketing labels.
