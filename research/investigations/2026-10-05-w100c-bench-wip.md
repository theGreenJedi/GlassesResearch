# W100C bench evaluation — W.I.P. — 2026-10-05

**Model:** `GLS-0245` — W100C  
**Status:** **W.I.P. — Work In Progress**  
**Evidence lane:** GlassesResearch direct observation and repeated bench testing unless otherwise labeled  
**Specimen scope:** one project-held W100C specimen; specimen-specific failures must not be generalized to all W100C units without corroboration  
**Report Card:** pending completion of bench evaluation

> **W.I.P. means the bench is active.** Findings below are published so owners and researchers can see what has actually been observed, while unresolved fields remain unresolved. This page will be revised as testing continues.

## Why this update is public now

GlassesResearch has moved beyond catalog identity for W100C and is now testing a physical specimen. The current unit has produced a strong, reproducible left/right electronics anomaly that is useful to disclose immediately, but the evaluation is not complete enough for a final Report Card score.

## Acquisition and vendor identity

The project-held specimen was purchased on Alibaba on **2026-08-22** from **Veni Venus (Shenzhen) Technology Co., Ltd.** The order identified the product as **“W100C AI Smart Glasses Wireless Enabled Call Music for Office Wearable Headset Smart Translation Glasses”**, model number **W100C**, color **Black**. The displayed unit price was **USD 21.39** and the displayed order total was **USD 37.13**.

This purchase record identifies the seller and the purchased market identity. It does **not** establish that Veni Venus is the original manufacturer or that every W100C sold by another vendor is electrically identical.

![Alibaba purchase record for the project-held W100C specimen](/images/research/w100c/w100c-alibaba-order.jpg)

## Physical inspection

Direct inspection confirms a metal-front / black-temple sunglasses form factor with adjustable nose pads, magnetic charging contacts, touch-marked temple areas, open-ear speakers, and two very small microphone slits. The microphone slits are on the underside, one on each side, and are difficult to photograph because they are extremely small and nearly the same color as the frame.

No camera aperture or display was observed during this inspection.

![Project-held W100C specimen during bench inspection](/images/research/w100c/w100c-bench-front.jpg)

The inner-temple markings include the smart-glasses regulatory legend and the magnetic charging-contact area.

## Indicator light and pairing state

The manual depicts an indicator light. With the glasses powered but unpaired, a visible indicator was confirmed on the **right temple only** during this session. It alternated/flashed **blue and red** while the glasses were attempting to pair.

No equivalent left-temple indicator was observed.

![Right-temple W100C pairing indicator illuminated blue during bench test](/images/research/w100c/w100c-bench-pairing-led.jpg)

When left unpaired, the specimen stopped attempting to pair and powered off after approximately **90 seconds** during the observed test.

## Bluetooth identity and reconnect behavior

With the glasses off, the phone's Bluetooth discovery list did not show the W100 entries associated with this specimen. After the glasses were powered on, **two separate entries named `W100`** appeared in Android's pairing UI: one presented as an audio/headset-style endpoint and one as a general Bluetooth entry.

The audio/headset `W100` endpoint paired successfully. Access to contacts and call history was declined. Immediately after pairing, the glasses announced **“Connected”** through the physical right-side speaker.

The second `W100` entry did not pair successfully through Android Settings in this test and returned a **“Can't communicate with …”** message. Its role has not yet been established. GlassesResearch is therefore **not** labeling one endpoint BLE and the other Bluetooth Classic without protocol-level evidence.

![Android discovery showing the two W100 entries observed from the powered specimen](/images/research/w100c/w100c-bluetooth-two-endpoints.jpg)

After successful pairing:

- powering the glasses off by folding the right temple and powering them back on produced a spoken **“Connected”** message after roughly a second;
- disabling Bluetooth on the phone produced a spoken **“Disconnected”** message;
- re-enabling phone Bluetooth after about 15 seconds produced an almost immediate spoken **“Connected”** message.

This establishes fast automatic reconnection to the previously paired phone on this specimen.

## Power behavior is right-temple dependent

The power behavior is asymmetric.

- Folding the **right temple** to roughly **45 degrees** powered the glasses off.
- Folding the **left temple** did **not** power the glasses off.
- Reopening/powering the unit after prior pairing restored the Bluetooth audio connection automatically.

This is consistent with the Hall-effect/hinge-triggered power behavior claimed for W100C, but the bench result specifically shows that the detected power trigger on this specimen is associated with the right side.

## Audio anomaly — repeatable on this specimen

The project-held specimen did **not** produce normal bilateral audio.

Observed repeatedly:

- ordinary music playback was audible through the **right physical temple speaker only**;
- the **left physical speaker remained silent**;
- a stereo balance/channel test announcing **LEFT** produced sound from the **right physical temple**;
- the same test announcing **RIGHT** produced **silence**;
- the result was repeated with a second channel test;
- telephone-call audio was likewise heard only from the right physical temple.

The strongest current interpretation is a **specimen-specific channel-routing and/or left-side electronics fault**. The present evidence does not establish whether the failure is the left speaker itself, its wiring/amplifier path, channel mapping in firmware, or another internal defect.

**Do not generalize this fault to all W100C glasses yet.**

## Microphone behavior

Two microphone slits were physically observed, one per side.

During a live call, the remote party reported that the glasses microphone path sounded **“tinny”** compared with the phone. The remote party could hear tapping on the **right temple** but did not hear comparable tapping on the **left temple**.

That demonstrates a working microphone path associated with the right side during this session. It does **not** yet establish whether the left microphone element is absent, electrically failed, ignored by firmware, or simply insufficiently coupled to the tap test.

## Touch controls

Touch areas are marked on both temples, but control behavior was also asymmetric.

### Right temple

The right touch surface was functional. During music playback it could:

- pause/resume playback;
- invoke the phone's assistant/action chooser with some multi-tap/press patterns;
- raise volume with approximately **4 successful attempts out of 5** in the repeated tap test;
- advance to the next track with approximately **2–3 successful attempts out of 5**.

Gesture recognition was less reliable for the more complex next-track command. A long press did not produce a reliable volume change during the tested sequence.

### Left temple

The left touch surface produced **no observed response** during the same session.

Combined with the silent left speaker and weak/no left-side tap pickup during the call test, this strengthens the case that the project-held specimen has a left-side electronics fault. It still does not identify the failed component.

## Current bench conclusion

The W100C platform itself is real, pairable, and capable of standard Bluetooth call/music use without granting contacts access. On the project-held specimen, however, several functions collapse onto the right side:

- power sensing;
- visible pairing/status LED;
- demonstrated audio output;
- demonstrated microphone pickup;
- working touch control.

The left side has a physical speaker, a microphone slit and a marked touch area, but none of those left-side functions has yet been demonstrated electrically on this specimen.

That asymmetry is the primary bench finding so far.

## Still open

The W.I.P. tag remains until the outstanding bench work is complete. Remaining work includes charging/runtime characterization, protocol/BLE characterization, remaining GREP fields, repeatable control mapping, and internal inspection/teardown only if warranted by the research plan.

The final GlassesResearch report, Report Card and completed GREP packet will replace this W.I.P. state when the evaluation is finished.

## Evidence boundary

- **Directly observed / repeated:** physical layout, pairing LED location/color behavior, dual `W100` discovery entries, successful audio-endpoint pairing, reconnect behavior, right-hinge power behavior, right-only reproduced audio, stereo channel anomaly, right functional touch surface, left non-responsive touch surface, call-path microphone observation.
- **Primary/commercial evidence:** Goodway W100C identity/specifications and the Alibaba purchase record.
- **Not yet established:** whether the left-side defect is common to W100C, exact internal failure mode, exact role/protocol of the second `W100` Bluetooth entry, battery/runtime performance, or final Report Card scores.
