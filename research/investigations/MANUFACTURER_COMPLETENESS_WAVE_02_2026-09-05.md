# Manufacturer Completeness Audit — Wave 02

**Date:** 2026-09-05  
**Scope:** Vuzix, XREAL / Nreal, Meta eyewear, Snap Spectacles, Solos  
**Program:** [Manufacturer Completeness](../../docs/MANUFACTURER_COMPLETENESS.md)

## Result

Wave 02 finds **23 defensible canonical eyewear admissions** and **four adjacent head-worn devices**, while completing whole-manufacturer reconciliation for Meta and Snap without inflating their counts.

The large count effect is overwhelmingly historical Vuzix display eyewear. This is not a change in GlassesResearch scope: the existing catalog already admits host-driven/tethered display glasses from XREAL, VITURE, Rokid, RayNeo and others. Vuzix's earlier AV/Wrap/STAR products were manufacturer-described video eyewear worn on eyeglass-style temples and sold into consumer/developer channels. Excluding them solely because they predate the modern XR-glasses market would be inconsistent.

## Canonical admissions

### Vuzix legacy display-eyewear branch

Vuzix SEC filings establish that its consumer binocular Video Eyewear products were mounted on frames with eyeglass-style temples and sold as mobile/private displays. The 2009 filing names four then-current models: **AV230 XL, AV310 widescreen, AV920 and VR920**. Vuzix also records a monocular **M920** line sold from 2003 through 2009.

The Wrap family, introduced in fall 2009, is explicitly described by Vuzix as its fourth generation of binocular Video Eyewear. First-party filings identify current/sold models across the family: **Wrap 230, Wrap 310XL, Wrap 920, Wrap 920VR, Wrap 920AR, Wrap 1200, Wrap 1200VR, STAR 1200**, followed by HDMI-era **Wrap 1200DX, Wrap 1200DX VR, Wrap 1200DX-AR and STAR 1200DX**. Vuzix states the 2013/2014 Video Eyewear/VR products were sold through its own web store, retailers including Amazon and Macy's, distributors, and international web stores; its DX AR models were purchased directly from Vuzix.

These are materially named hardware identities rather than colors or lens packages. VR/AR variants add tracking and, for AR, cameras or see-through optics.

### Vuzix M400-C

Vuzix introduced **M400-C** in January 2022 as a distinct M-Series smart-glasses product. Initial units had already shipped, general availability was scheduled for Q2, and Vuzix priced it at $1,199. M400-C preserves the M400 eyeglass-mounted camera/display module but removes the onboard Android computer in favor of a USB-C host architecture. That ownership/compute boundary is materially different enough to require its own record.

Primary evidence:
- https://ir.vuzix.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1937/vuzix-introduces-its-new-m400-c-smart-glasses

Admission: `GLS-0212`.

## Solos

The previous canonical history began at AirGo 2. Whole-company reconstruction exposes a pre-AirGo HUD lineage plus a missing first AirGo generation and the current fifth-generation A5.

### Solos Smart Glasses — first HUD generation

Solos/Kopin announced the original performance HUD eyewear in 2016. A USA Cycling purchase path offered preorder access in May 2016 at $499; Kopin later reported completing shipment of the first generation. The product used a see-through pupil-scale HUD and was architecturally unlike later display-free AirGo audio glasses.

Primary evidence:
- https://www.kopin.com/press-releases/solos-and-usa-cycling-partner-ahead-of-rio-2016-olympic-games/
- https://www.kopin.com/press-releases/kopin-provides-business-update-and-second-quarter-2017-operating-results/
- https://solosglasses.com/blogs/news/solos-smart-glasses-hit-their-kickstarter-target

Admission: `GLS-0214`.

### Solos Smart Glasses — second HUD generation

Kopin formally called the CES 2018 product a **new generation** of SOLOS Smart Glasses, described it as building on the first generation, and announced Q1 2018 availability at $499. In May 2018 Kopin reported that the new generation had begun shipping and was available through its ecommerce infrastructure.

Primary evidence:
- https://www.kopin.com/press-releases/solos-announces-new-generation-smart-performance-glasses-at-ces-2018/
- https://www.kopin.com/press-releases/kopin-provides-business-update-and-first-quarter-2018-operating-results/

Admission: `GLS-0215`.

### Solos AirGo 1

The first AirGo generation is preserved in a 2021 Solos user manual and the 2020 AirGo crowdfunding program. It is a display-free Bluetooth smart-glasses architecture with interchangeable frames/temples and phone-hosted assistant/fitness functions. This is the missing predecessor required to make the existing AirGo 2 record coherent.

Evidence:
- https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/solostechnology/solos-smart-glasses-wellbeing-and-fashion-styles-all-in-one/faqs
- surviving Solos AirGo 1 user manual, dated 2021

Admission: `GLS-0216`.

### Solos AirGo A5

Solos currently labels **AirGo A5** its **5th Generation of Solos Audio AI Smartglasses** and exposes a $249 manufacturer purchase/backorder path. A5 is therefore a distinct named generation, not a frame style.

Primary evidence:
- https://solosglasses.com/products/hydro-x-smartglasses-solos-airgo%E2%84%A2-a5

Admission: `GLS-0217`.

## Meta completeness result — no new IDs

Meta's current first-party material describes **Meta Glasses** as one collection available in three **styles**: Adventurer, Fury and the Kylie slim-oval/Starfire retail styling. This is style proliferation, not evidence for three electronics generations. GlassesResearch therefore retains `GLS-0165` as the existing canonical representative/purchaser-history record and preserves Fury/Starfire as sibling style identities in the Meta lineage rather than minting duplicate GLS IDs.

Primary evidence:
- https://www.meta.com/ai-glasses/smart-ai-glasses/
- https://www.meta.com/blog/introducing-meta-glasses-a-range-of-new-styles-from-meta-and-essilorluxottica-starting-at-299/

The existing Ray-Ban Stories, Ray-Ban Meta generations, Display branch and Oakley branch remain distinct canonical purchaser-history entries. This audit finds no missing historical Meta electronics generation that crosses the admission threshold.

## Snap completeness result — no new IDs

Snap's own surviving Spectacles 2 support material explicitly identifies **Nico, Veronica and Original** as Spectacles 2 frame variants. ## Process consequence

This wave reinforces why manufacturer completeness is distinct from model coverage. Vuzix already had eleven canonical records and one of the site's stronger lineage chapters, yet the historical audit uncovered an entire sold display-eyewear era. Meta and Snap demonstrate the opposite outcome: a deep audit can also prove that attractive new names are merely styles/variants and should not inflate the count.

The correct success metric is therefore **resolved manufacturer history**, not number of new IDs.
