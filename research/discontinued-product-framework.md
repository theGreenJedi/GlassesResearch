# Discontinued Product Preservation Framework

A discontinued device is still part of the smart-glasses record. GlassesResearch should preserve what the product was, what survives, why it stopped being useful, and what it contributed.

## Lifecycle record

For each discontinued or abandoned model, capture when evidence permits:

- launch date and region;
- launch MSRP;
- last known sale or discontinuation date;
- manufacturer and lineage;
- companion application and account requirements;
- cloud/service shutdown date and scope;
- firmware and application availability;
- remaining offline/local functionality;
- remaining standard connectivity such as Bluetooth, USB, Wi-Fi, DisplayPort, or audio profiles;
- prescription and replacement-part situation;
- known aging/failure patterns with sample scope;
- community software, archived SDKs, firmware, documentation, teardowns, and repair information;
- historical contribution or notable first;
- evidence IDs and last verification date.

## Service status

Use descriptive states rather than a single dead/alive flag:

- Supported: vendor service and normal support remain active.
- Maintenance: product is no longer current but essential services/support remain available.
- Discontinued-functional: sales/support ended, but core advertised functions remain usable.
- Degraded: meaningful functions were lost through shutdown, app loss, account failure, or incompatible software.
- Community-sustained: important functionality survives primarily through community tooling or archived software.
- Offline-only: network/vendor functions are gone but useful local functions remain.
- Nonfunctional: normal use is no longer practical without unusual restoration work.
- Unknown: current state has not been verified.

## Failure mode

Do not write only that a product died. Identify the mechanism where evidence supports it: commercial discontinuation, cloud shutdown, mandatory app removal, account-server loss, certificate/API breakage, battery aging, proprietary accessory loss, firmware incompatibility, physical failure, or another cause.

## Survival assessment

The preservation record should feed Cloud Independence and Owner Control. Ask what a second-hand owner can still do today without privileged vendor assistance: boot, pair, record, display, play audio, transfer files, use sensors, install software, change endpoints, repair, and develop for the device.

## Preservation priorities

Before links disappear, preserve stable references to manuals, SDK documentation, source repositories, regulatory filings, application package/version information, firmware references, protocol documentation, teardowns, accessory specifications, and credible shutdown notices. Respect copyright and licensing; preserve metadata and lawful archival references when redistribution is not permitted.

## Historical interpretation

A failed commercial product can still be technically important. Historical contribution should be recorded separately from present-day value. Likewise, nostalgia should not inflate current functionality or report-card scores.

## Cemetery output

The eventual Discontinued Product Cemetery should be a navigable research index, not a joke page: model, lineage, active years, service status, what still works, primary failure mode, preservation assets, and historical significance, with links to the full device record and evidence.
