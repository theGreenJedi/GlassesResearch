# Requirements-Driven Buyer Framework

GlassesResearch should not begin with a universal ranking. Begin with what the wearer needs.

## 1. Is visual output required?
If no, compare camera/audio AI glasses first. If yes, determine whether the need is simple text/HUD information, a large virtual display, or spatially anchored AR.

## 2. Is environmental vision required?
A display-only viewer and a camera-equipped contextual assistant solve different problems. Verify camera access and actual visual-AI behavior rather than relying on an AI label.

## 3. Must it accept prescription lenses?
Distinguish direct prescription glazing from inserts. Check prescription range, progressive support, replacement availability and whether an ordinary optician can service the frame.

## 4. Must core functions survive without vendor cloud service?
Prefer devices with documented local capture, standard Bluetooth/USB behavior, retrievable applications/firmware and owner-accessible interfaces. Use the Survival Matrix rather than assuming Bluetooth equals independence.

## 5. Is developer or owner access important?
Check SDK/API maturity, sensor access, display access, sideloading, BLE visibility, custom endpoints and community tooling. Openness and Owner Control are separate questions.

## 6. What privacy constraints apply?
Check recording indicators, microphones, cloud handling, accounts, permissions and institutional restrictions before purchase.

## 7. What is the acceptable form factor?
Separate ordinary-looking glasses from conspicuous HUD/AR hardware. Weight alone does not capture comfort; balance, temples, bridge and heat matter.

## 8. What is the real budget?
Use total ownership cost: device, prescription lenses, required compute puck/phone, subscription, accessories and likely replacement components. Compare current/refurbished/used pricing with MSRP.

## Decision output
The eventual interactive selector should return a small set of candidates and explain *why* each survived the user's requirements. A device should be excluded explicitly when a hard requirement is not met or remains unknown.
