# EV-0085 — WIRED cross-model smart-glasses field review

- **Evidence state:** attributed independent review
- **Last verified:** 2026-08-24
- **Scope:** category boundaries, fit, display usability, controls, software maturity and privacy

## Source

- WIRED, “The Best Smart Glasses That Aren't Just a Gimmick”: https://www.wired.com/gallery/best-smart-glasses/

## Supported use

WIRED's current guide is useful as a set of attributed, hands-on observations across product categories that are often collapsed into one “smart glasses” market. The review distinguishes camera/audio glasses, sports glasses, camera-free information displays and tethered wearable displays.

Specific observations worth investigating include display alignment and edge blur, the need to reposition a frame, dependence on a light shade, discomfort during display adjustment, control-device latency, immature spatial software, and the privacy implications of both bystander capture and wearer data handling.

These observations support new **test questions**, not transferred scores:

- Is the display readable throughout the usable eyebox for different face geometry?
- Must the wearer reposition the frame to obtain a sharp image?
- Is a shade required under ordinary bright conditions?
- Does extended display use create discomfort or headache?
- Are controls responsive and usable without breaking the interaction?
- Is the enabling software stable enough for the advertised mode?
- What signals tell bystanders that sensing or recording is active?
- What app, cloud and media-permission controls are available to the wearer?

## Claim boundary

WIRED's device judgments are independent editorial observations, not manufacturer specifications and not GlassesResearch hands-on findings. They must remain attributed, and they do not by themselves change a Core Report Card score.

The guide also appears internally inconsistent in describing Even Realities G2 as having neither cameras nor microphones while elsewhere discussing conversation capture and listing microphone-related behavior. GlassesResearch's first-party record supports a camera-free design with microphones. The “no microphones” wording is therefore excluded from reusable evidence pending correction or clarification.

Affiliate links are disclosed by the publication. Product selection and ranking remain editorial choices rather than a reproducible cross-category benchmark.
