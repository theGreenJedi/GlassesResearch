## Verified product boundary

Snap introduced the 2021 Spectacles as its first glasses to display augmented reality directly to the wearer. They were distributed to selected AR creators rather than sold at retail. That creator-program route establishes real-world developer access without converting the device into a normal consumer product.

Snap documents dual 3D waveguide displays, a 26.3-degree field of view, up to 2,000-nit dynamic brightness, 15 ms motion-to-photon latency, Snapdragon XR1 compute, two RGB cameras, four microphones, stereo speakers, a touchpad, 6DoF tracking, hand/marker/surface tracking, 134 g weight and approximately 30 minutes of operation per charge.

## Software and ownership boundary

Lens Studio was the supported development environment. Creators could build Lenses and push them wirelessly to Spectacles for rapid testing. Snap also documented Scan, Voice Scan, a Lens Carousel and capture of 10-second Snaps with AR overlays.

This is a substantial supported creative-development surface, but it is not evidence of general-purpose application sideloading, open firmware, an unlocked boot chain or owner-selected system software. The platform's durable usefulness is therefore tied more closely to Snap's Lens Studio and service ecosystem than an open Android or documented low-level hardware platform.
