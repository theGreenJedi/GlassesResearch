# EV-0083 — Smart-glasses visual acuity and eye-strain study

- **Evidence state:** peer-reviewed longitudinal observational research
- **Last verified:** 2026-08-24
**Scope:** logistics and order-picking employees

## Source

- Herold et al., Sensors 24(20), 6515: https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/24/20/6515
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/s24206515

## Supported findings

The study followed 43 employees at two German companies over six months. Group-level before/after-shift eyesight differences at baseline were small and not statistically significant, while some individuals experienced deterioration, particularly at near distances. Participants over 40 had higher reported odds of deterioration, and eye fatigue, rubbing, and burning were common complaints.

## Claim boundary

This observational workplace study does not prove that smart glasses generally damage eyesight, nor does it establish safety or risk for every display architecture. Device, workload, exposure, age, prescription, and fit matter. GlassesResearch should use it to justify long-duration comfort and visual-fatigue testing—not to issue a universal hazard claim.
