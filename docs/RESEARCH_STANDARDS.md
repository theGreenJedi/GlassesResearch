# Research Standards

GlassesResearch exists to make smart-glasses information easier to verify, challenge, preserve, and reuse. The public site should favor useful information over internal process, but the process behind factual claims must remain inspectable.

## Public claim states

Public pages use three simple states:

- **Verified** — sufficient evidence supports the claim and the supporting source is available.
- **Unknown** — adequate evidence has not yet been located.
- **Disputed** — credible evidence conflicts or a substantive challenge remains unresolved.

The source itself explains whether evidence came from a manufacturer, regulator, academic paper, repository, independent reporting, community work, or hands-on testing.

## Source preference

When several sources exist, prefer the source closest to the underlying fact: official technical documentation, regulatory filings, release notes, repositories, standards documents, peer-reviewed or primary research, and direct hands-on observations. Independent reporting is valuable when it adds verification or context. Marketplace copy, affiliate pages, unsourced summaries, and repeated claims are not treated as independent confirmation.

A manufacturer claim may establish what the manufacturer says; it does not automatically establish real-world behavior outside the conditions of that claim.

## Unknown is a research state, not filler

An unknown field should remain **Unknown** rather than being guessed, inferred from omission, or replaced by a generic statement. Unknowns are candidates for future research. When verification is presently impossible, the record should say why when that context is useful.

## Conflicting evidence

Conflicts are preserved rather than silently reconciled. When two credible sources disagree, the claim becomes **Disputed** until the discrepancy can be resolved. The resolution should retain enough history to explain what changed and why.

## Corrections and challenges

Every factual claim is challengeable. The [Research Challenge process](RESEARCH_CHALLENGES.md) provides a public route to submit stronger evidence, request correction, or dispute an interpretation. Substantive challenges should have a traceable resolution rather than disappearing through an unlogged edit.

Possible outcomes include corrected, clarified, upheld, needs more evidence, or superseded.

## Evidence before structure

Public resource pages should link concrete, identifiable resources. Generic instructions such as “search Reddit,” “check Discord,” or “look on GitHub” are research tasks for maintainers, not finished resources for visitors.

The standing workflow is:

**Discover → Verify → Preserve → Organize → Cross-reference**

## Structured data follows visible evidence

Machine-readable metadata is another representation of the public research, not a place to make stronger claims. Product identity, model, brand, category, descriptions, ratings, reviews, prices, availability, and offers may be marked up only when the same information is visibly supported on the page and satisfies the relevant format requirements.

A multi-axis GlassesResearch Report Card is not silently converted into a single aggregate star rating. Review or rating markup should be added only when the site publishes a defensible corresponding review or overall rating for that exact product. Unknown prices and availability remain unknown rather than being supplied merely to qualify for a rich result.

## Preservation

Where law, licensing, and practicality allow, fragile technical material should be preserved with provenance and integrity metadata. A preservation record is not permission to redistribute material whose distribution is restricted.

## Independence

GlassesResearch does not sell rankings, sponsorship placement, or affiliate-driven winners. Evidence should be evaluated by its ability to support a claim, not by whether it favors a manufacturer, project, community, or preferred technology.

## Editorial rule

The public-facing rule is simple:

> Information first. Infrastructure second.

Internal schemas, validators, IDs, workflows, and preservation machinery exist to support clear answers. They should not crowd the reader unless they are necessary to verify or reuse the research.
