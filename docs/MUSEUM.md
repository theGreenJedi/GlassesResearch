# The GlassesResearch Museum

The Museum preserves the history of smart glasses and wearable computing as a living technical collection.

It is not limited to successful products or devices that remain available for purchase. Discontinued systems, failed experiments, developer editions, industrial wearables, obscure OEM designs, rebadged products, and abandoned platforms all belong in the historical record.

## What the Museum preserves

The collection is intended to include:

- early wearable-computing and head-mounted-display systems;
- enterprise and industrial smart glasses;
- consumer augmented-reality and media-display glasses;
- camera, audio, translation, accessibility, and AI-native glasses;
- developer kits, prototypes, announced products, and canceled projects;
- discontinued devices and services;
- ODM reference designs, white-label families, and rebadged models;
- manuals, specifications, software history, SDKs, firmware references, regulatory records, photographs, teardowns, repairs, and community discoveries.

## A model belongs even when little is known

A museum entry may begin with only a name, approximate date, manufacturer, photograph, listing, or regulatory record. Unresolved identity and incomplete evidence should be labeled clearly rather than excluded.

As research improves, each entry can grow into a model dossier containing:

- identity and product genealogy;
- release and discontinuation history;
- technical specifications and components;
- software, applications, firmware, and cloud dependencies;
- development access and interoperability;
- known repairs, modifications, hacks, and recovery procedures;
- photographs, manuals, archived sources, and contributor attribution;
- evidence status, contradictions, and unanswered questions.

## Contribute to the Museum

Own an older or unusual device? Have packaging, photographs, manuals, software, repair notes, or memories of a discontinued platform?

[Visit the contribution portal](CONTRIBUTE.md) and help preserve it. Contributions may be credited by real name, handle, project name, or pseudonym—or submitted anonymously.
