# Meta faces biometric lawsuit over alleged NameTag training data

**Event date:** September 4, 2026  
**Status:** Verified procedural fact; underlying biometric and training claims remain allegations

A proposed class action, **Alvarez v. Meta Platforms, Inc., No. 1:26-cv-10773**, was filed September 4, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois, Eastern Division.

The complaint alleges that Meta collected or used facial images and biometric information associated with Facebook and Instagram to develop or test the unreleased **NameTag** face-recognition system for smart glasses and generative-AI systems including Emu and Muse Image. GlassesResearch does **not** treat those allegations as established fact.

Independent reporting before the suit documented that NameTag-related face-recognition code had appeared in the Meta AI companion app and was later removed. Meta publicly disputed characterizations that the unreleased feature existed as a consumer product and described the work as exploratory. Those technical reports do not by themselves prove the complaint's training-data allegations.

## Why this matters

The filing creates a durable privacy and owner/bystander-governance research record for smart glasses. It also exposed a GlassesResearch discovery failure: a benchmark source produced a material smart-glasses story that the normal discovery configuration did not preserve promptly.

## Evidence boundary

Verified here:
- a federal complaint was filed;
- the court, case number and filing date above;
- the complaint makes biometric/training allegations concerning NameTag and Meta AI systems;
- prior independent reporting documented NameTag-related code in the companion app and its later removal.

Not verified as fact:
- that Meta unlawfully collected biometric identifiers;
- that Facebook or Instagram photos were in fact used as alleged to train NameTag, Emu or Muse Image;
- liability, damages, class certification or the merits of any pleaded claim.

Those remain allegations unless established by stronger evidence or later adjudication.
