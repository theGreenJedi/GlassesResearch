# MemoMind One enters the canonical catalog as GLS-0160

**Published:** August 18, 2026  
**Status:** Verified

MemoMind One has crossed the GlassesResearch documented-acquisition threshold and is now part of the canonical purchaser-history catalog as **GLS-0160**.

## What changed

A collector lead about MemoMind One was checked against current MemoMind and XGIMI primary material. MemoMind currently presents the product as camera-free dual-eye display AI glasses, lists a starting price of **$399**, and routes acquisition through Kickstarter.

Under the existing GlassesResearch purchaser-history rule, a documented paid preorder or crowdfunding route is enough to move a product from announcement-only status into the canonical acquisition ledger. MemoMind One therefore enters the catalog with state **preorder/crowdfunding**.

The canonical purchaser-history ledger now contains **159 active models or explicitly marketed product generations**.

## What we verified

Current manufacturer material describes:

- a camera-free design;
- dual-eye Micro-LED displays;
- open-ear audio and microphones;
- prescription-lens options;
- AI-assisted recording and summarization;
- translation and teleprompter functions;
- a current starting price of **$399**;
- an acquisition route through Kickstarter.

Earlier XGIMI launch material used **Memo One** for the flagship product, while current manufacturer material uses **MemoMind One**. GlassesResearch therefore preserves the earlier name as launch-era lineage/alias evidence rather than treating it as a separate product.

## What we are not claiming

Canonical identity does **not** establish broad purchaser fulfillment, independently measured battery life or brightness, a public SDK, owner-controlled firmware, bootloader or recovery access, local AI execution, cloud independence, or ordinary-optician serviceability. Those remain unknown until evidence supports them.

## Why it matters

MemoMind One is a useful example of why the catalog distinguishes announcement from documented acquisition. A product can be technically interesting before buyers have a real path to obtain it; once that path exists, it becomes materially more relevant for comparison, tracking, and future owner-control research.

Its camera-free architecture is also significant in a market where social acceptance and privacy concerns increasingly shape how wearable displays are evaluated.
