# Ray-Ban Meta: unexpected voice-activity logs and an unresolved owner-control question

**Research note:** October 25, 2026  
**Across the Wire · Community-reported / independently reported company response · Not independently tested by GlassesResearch**

## What owners reported

On [October 13, 2026, a Ray-Ban Meta owner wrote in r/RaybanMeta](https://www.reddit.com/r/RaybanMeta/comments/1x4n96n/warning_my_rayban_meta_glasses_are_recording/) that conversations appeared as audio recordings and transcripts in the Meta AI companion app **without intentional voice activation**. The poster says the **“Respond without Hey Meta”** option was disabled and describes seeing the glasses' white status light turn on while speaking. They report observing the behavior on **three pairs**, described as second- and third-generation devices, after a firmware update. These are **owner statements**, not GlassesResearch reproduction, and the timing does not establish the update as the cause.

Other commenters in that discussion describe unexpected indicator-light activation or entries in the voice-activity log. Some describe unexpected activation **without** confirming that audio was saved. Those are distinct observations: an illuminated light, a mistaken wake event, a local voice-history entry, a stored transcript, and a network upload are **not interchangeable claims**.

## What Meta said — and what remains disputed

[Ars Technica reported October 16](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/10/ray-ban-meta-smart-glasses-found-recording-audio-without-wearers-consent/) that Meta told Android Authority the issue **“has been resolved”** and said owners can review and delete voice logs. The company response as quoted in that reporting did **not** identify the root cause, affected hardware/firmware builds, fix mechanism, number of affected devices, or a public reproducibility test. Ars also reported that Android Authority could not reproduce the problem before Meta's resolution statement.

In a **later reply within the original Reddit thread**, the reporting owner said a further update had not stopped the behavior on their glasses. Other commenters continued reporting unusual indicator or log behavior. These reports conflict with the broad reading of Meta's resolution statement, but **do not independently establish an ongoing defect across production devices**. The observed state is **disputed / not independently verified**; it is not accurate to announce either fleet-wide failure or proven fleet-wide resolution.

## Owner-control and research implications

The material research question is whether the eyewear and companion app enforce the owner's chosen activation boundary: **what triggers voice processing, what is stored, where it is stored, and how the wearer can audit or delete it**. No available evidence here proves continuous ambient recording, camera capture, server-side upload, a deliberate surveillance feature, or any particular exploit. The discussion concerns **reported voice-activity recordings and transcripts**, not demonstrated video recording while asleep.

**Follow-up evidence sought:** exact glasses generation, hardware and firmware versions, companion-app version, voice-activation settings, timestamped activity-log entries, reproducible trigger conditions, and a dated vendor fix identifier. With appropriate consent and privacy safeguards, independent device tests could distinguish inadvertent activation from logging or transport behavior. Do not collect or publish bystanders' private audio as part of this investigation.

**Disposition:** Retain as an attributed privacy/owner-control investigation; do not mark the behavior as GlassesResearch Verified, claim a known affected population, or score a specific device until evidence supports it.
