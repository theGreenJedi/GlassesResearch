# New York courts prohibit recording-capable smart glasses in UCS facilities

**Published:** July 14, 2026  
**Discovered by GlassesResearch:** August 28, 2026  
**Policy effective:** July 20, 2026  
**Status:** Verified primary-source policy change

The New York State Unified Court System published a statewide policy prohibiting smart glasses inside UCS facilities, effective July 20, 2026. The court system defines covered devices broadly as eyewear or headwear containing a camera, microphone, computer, or other technology that permits video or audio recording, whether prescription or non-prescription.

The rule is not limited to parties or spectators in a courtroom. New York's own court materials state that covered smart glasses are prohibited inside UCS facilities. People arriving with them must use the system's safekeeping/vouchering process rather than entering while possessing the device.

## Why it matters

GlassesResearch already tracks a separate court restriction in England and Wales. New York now provides independent primary-source evidence that recording-capable smart glasses are receiving device-specific institutional rules in more than one jurisdiction.

That supports a **convergence signal**, not a universal conclusion. Two court systems do not establish that smart glasses are broadly prohibited in courts everywhere. They do establish that institutions are beginning to write policies specifically around eyewear that can sense, record, or compute.

The distinction also matters for product design and accessibility. A blanket category such as “smart glasses” can include camera-free assistive or display eyewear, while New York's operative definition focuses on technology capable of recording audio or video. Future policy research should preserve those exact scopes rather than reducing every institutional rule to “smart glasses banned.”
