# Victoria's 2027 school smart-glasses restrictions: scope and accessibility

**Published:** October 23, 2026

**Across the Wire · Primary-source documented · October 23, 2026 · Not an observation of enforcement**

Victoria, Australia has published smart-glasses restrictions for the 2027 school year. The distinction between the statewide student rule and the stricter government-school policy matters; this is **not** a claim that every adult at every Victorian school is already prohibited from wearing smart glasses.

## Two different rules

The [Victorian government's October 21 announcement](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/smart-glasses-be-banned-all-victorian-schools), [student-device guidance](https://www.vic.gov.au/mobile-phones-schools) and [Victorian Registration and Qualifications Authority's October 22 notice](https://www2.vrqa.vic.gov.au/new-minimum-standard-use-personal-electronic-devices-schools) establish a statewide student requirement: from the start of the 2027 school year, students in government, Catholic and independent schools must switch off and store smart glasses during school hours. Ministerial Order No. 1515 commences **January 28, 2027**.

A separate [Victorian Department of Education policy, updated October 20](https://www2.education.vic.gov.au/pal/smart-glasses-covert-filming-devices/policy), takes effect **January 27, 2027** and applies more broadly at **government schools**. Students, teachers, parents, carers, contractors, volunteers and visitors may not wear or use smart glasses on school premises or at school-organised activities, including offsite events. The policy applies on school grounds beyond school hours.

The October 21 announcement describes extending the adult rule to non-government schools as a **future legislative commitment contingent on re-election**, not an existing universal adult ban.

## Camera-free glasses are not categorically exempt

The department defines smart glasses as spectacles capable of sending, receiving, storing, recording or processing digital information. That definition does **not** require a camera. It may therefore cover camera-free display, audio, translation and accessibility glasses. Whether a particular device qualifies requires applying the published definition; absence of a camera alone does not exclude it.

## Disability exemption and criticism

The department permits a documented exemption where smart glasses are a reasonable adjustment for disability, supported by evidence, and **no other device or approach can reasonably provide the adjustment**. The policy explicitly gives vision impairment as an example.

In an [October 21 public statement](https://www.nationaltribune.com.au/vision-australia-concerned-by-victorian-schools-smart-glasses-ban/), **Vision Australia** argued that this last-resort requirement creates barriers for blind and low-vision students, questioned its effect on established assistive eyewear, and criticized the absence of disability-community consultation. This is an attributed advocacy position, not an independently adjudicated finding.

## Research significance and evidence limits

The rules illustrate a critical distinction for wearable research: **camera absence does not necessarily confer institutional acceptability**. GlassesResearch should distinguish device capabilities, bystander-privacy rationale, actual policy language and accessibility accommodation instead of treating every school rule as a ban on covert cameras alone.
