# Smart-Glasses Purchaser-History Census — August 2026

## Snapshot finding

As of **August 21, 2026**, the GlassesResearch canonical purchaser-history ledger contained **162 distinct smart-glasses models or explicitly marketed product generations** that had crossed a documented route to acquisition.

That number is an **evidence floor**, not a claim that exactly 162 smart-glasses products have ever existed. Obscure regional products and poorly archived historical devices can still be discovered later. The live count therefore grows only when evidence supports another admission or changes when an identity correction is documented.

The current live ledger is [The List — Smart-Glasses Models Past and Present](/models/THE_LIST/). This page preserves the August 21, 2026 snapshot so the **162-model figure remains citable as a dated finding even after the live catalog changes**.

## What counts

A canonical row requires a named product identity plus evidence that the product crossed from concept into a real acquisition path. Qualifying paths include:

- public retail sale;
- a paid preorder or crowdfunding offer tied to a named product;
- documented enterprise procurement;
- documented developer-program procurement where hardware could actually be obtained.

A row does **not** imply that the product is still supported, was delivered in every region, remains purchasable today, or is recommended.

## What does not count

The purchaser-history census deliberately excludes:

- prototypes, concept devices, and reference designs that never reached a documented acquisition route;
- announced or previewed products before a real order/procurement path exists;
- unnamed partner devices whose final market identity is unresolved;
- frame colors, lens packages, storage bundles, and prescription variants when the underlying electronics generation is unchanged;
- co-brands or retail aliases that do not establish materially distinct hardware.

Those products can still appear in the broader [Smart-Glasses Model Registry](/models/CATALOG/) while they await evidence or identity resolution.

## Why use an acquisition threshold

Smart-glasses histories often mix shipped products, enterprise equipment, developer hardware, prototypes, cancelled concepts, and marketing previews into one list. That makes simple market counts difficult to compare.

GlassesResearch uses **documented acquisition** as the boundary because it is independently testable: either evidence exists that a named product could be obtained, or it does not yet. This does not make the ledger complete, but it gives every admitted record the same minimum historical meaning.

## Interpretation boundary

The **162-model figure is not** a shipment estimate, active-installed-base estimate, market-size estimate, or count of devices currently for sale. It is a dated count of distinct product identities and marketed hardware generations for which GlassesResearch had documented an acquisition route by August 21, 2026.
