# Resource Preservation

Useful smart-glasses knowledge often disappears: firmware links expire, vendor documentation moves, applications vanish from stores, community projects are abandoned, and support pages are rewritten.

GlassesResearch treats preservation as part of research rather than an afterthought.

## Preserve when practical

Candidates include manuals, firmware metadata, SDK documentation, public APIs, regulatory filings, release notes, companion applications, reverse-engineering notes, packet captures, tools, repair documentation, and other technical artifacts with long-term research value.

## Required provenance

A preservation record should identify:

- original source and URL;
- artifact or document title;
- associated model, company, or project;
- version and publication/release date when known;
- date last verified;
- license or redistribution constraints when known;
- integrity metadata such as a cryptographic hash when a lawful local copy is preserved;
- replacement, superseded, or dead-link status when applicable.

## Legal and ethical boundary

Preservation does not override copyright, licensing, privacy, access controls, export restrictions, or other legal obligations. When redistribution is not appropriate, GlassesResearch can preserve metadata, provenance, hashes, citations, and retrieval notes without publishing the restricted artifact itself.

Credentials, personal data, proprietary secrets, and unlawfully obtained material do not belong in the archive.

## Archive-first principle

When a lawful, fragile resource is likely to disappear, preserve enough information to identify and validate it before spending time on perfect organization.

Then apply the broader workflow:

**Discover → Verify → Preserve → Organize → Cross-reference**
