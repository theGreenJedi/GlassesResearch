# Lineage Research — ODG R-Series

**Research date:** 2026-08-12

This packet applies the lineage-first protocol: investigate the whole ODG R-series together before deciding which devices belong in the purchasable-model ledger and before assigning report-card scores.

## Lineage finding

Osterhout Design Group (ODG) developed multiple generations of self-contained Android smartglasses. The best-documented late commercial/development lineage is R-7 → R-8/R-9. Earlier R-series hardware also existed, but model-level public acquisition evidence is less consistent and remains archival work rather than something to infer from later generations.

### R-7 — admit to THE_LIST

R-7 crossed the repository's acquisition threshold. Contemporaneous ODG material described it as shipping from late 2015 for enterprise/industrial users, and contemporary reporting records a $2,750 price. It was a self-contained enterprise AR computer rather than a tethered display.

Documented characteristics include Qualcomm Snapdragon-class mobile compute, Android/ODG ReticleOS, binocular see-through display, cameras/sensors, audio, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth and an application-development story based on Android plus ODG tooling. Its industrial form factor and mass substantially limit ordinary-eyewear wearability, while standalone operation gives it strong cloud independence for local applications.

### R-8 — do not admit yet

ODG announced R-8 at CES 2017 as its smaller consumer/early-adopter model, powered by Snapdragon 835, with greater-than-40-degree FOV and a target price below $1,000. Manufacturer/Qualcomm material described expected availability in the second half of 2017, including developer units.

The current evidence package establishes announcement, intended price and intended availability, but not a sufficiently clean completed public acquisition route or shipment record under the repository's purchasing-history rule. R-8 therefore remains a documented product/development lead, not silently promoted into THE_LIST.

### R-9 — do not admit yet

R-9 was announced alongside R-8 as the higher-end prosumer/light-enterprise/development platform, using Snapdragon 835, a roughly 50-degree FOV 1080p platform, 6DoF-related Snapdragon VR capabilities and planned Vuforia support. Contemporary reporting gave an intended Q2 2017 launch and approximately $1,799 pricing.

As with R-8, announcement and planned commercial terms are not enough to prove that a qualifying acquisition route actually matured before ODG's collapse. R-9 stays outside the purchasable ledger pending stronger archival evidence of sale/procurement or delivered developer access.

### Common-ruler rationale

- **Hardware 7.0:** unusually capable self-contained wearable compute for its period, but bulky, power/thermal constrained and far behind the catalog's modern hardware ceiling.
- **Wearability 3.5:** purpose-built industrial eyewear, visibly bulky and heavy compared with ordinary glasses.
- **Visual AI N/A:** cameras and AR sensing existed, but the evidence does not establish a native visual-AI system comparable to the catalog's AI-glasses dimension.
- **Software 7.0:** Android/ReticleOS and an application platform made R-7 materially more programmable than appliance-style glasses; the dead vendor ecosystem limits the score today.
- **Openness 6.0:** developer access was meaningful, but this was not open hardware/firmware and does not approach the Monocle/Frame 10-level benchmark.
- **Owner Control 7.5:** standalone Android application execution offered substantial owner/developer choice relative to vendor-assistant-bound consumer glasses.
- **Cloud Independence 9.0:** core local AR/application operation did not inherently require ODG cloud services; the device was a standalone computer.
- **Hackability 6.5:** Android and developer tooling create a useful experimentation surface, but proprietary hardware/firmware and vanished support cap practical hackability.
- **Display/HUD 7.5:** binocular see-through AR was ambitious and useful, but optical quality, bulk and field-of-view limitations remain well below current best-in-catalog display systems.
- **Value 4.5:** the contemporaneous ~$2,750 enterprise price bought unusually advanced capability but was expensive even within professional smartglasses.
