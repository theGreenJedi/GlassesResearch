# Evidence-derived profiles — Chamelo

## Dusk Classic

Dusk Classic is smart eyewear in the literal sense: the intelligence is in the lens and audio system rather than in a camera or AI assistant. Its electrochromic tint can be adjusted electronically, with optional app/audio functionality, while the frame remains close to normal sunglasses. That makes it unusually wearable and cloud-independent but not especially open or hackable; there is no evidence of a developer platform comparable with open smart-glasses projects.

## Music Shield Gen 2

Music Shield Gen 2 pushes the same adaptive-lens idea into sport eyewear and adds open-ear audio. Chamelo explicitly presents it as a second generation and documents upgraded directional sound, revised fit/durability, 51 g weight, fast tint transitions and independent tint versus Bluetooth control. The result is a very owner-friendly appliance: its headline function still works without forcing account/cloud dependence, even though the electronics remain proprietary.

## Aura

Aura is a separate Prismatic/color-changing branch rather than a Music Shield generation. Its electronically selectable lens colors, 35 g form and prescription-ready configurations make it one of the more ordinary-looking pieces of smart eyewear in the catalog. It has little resemblance to an AR display, but it is valuable evidence that wearable augmentation can live in the optical material itself rather than in cameras, displays or assistants.
