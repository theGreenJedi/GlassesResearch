# Lineage Research — Pivothead Camera Eyewear

**Research date:** 2026-08-12

This packet applies the lineage-first protocol to Pivothead's camera-eyewear family. The public record supports two principal hardware generations rather than treating frame/color names as separate models: first-generation Pivothead video-recording eyewear and the later Pivothead SMART / Architect Edition platform.

## Lineage finding

Pivothead's early products were camera sunglasses built around the same core recording platform and sold under style/model names such as Recon, Aurora, Durango, and Moab. Those style names are not treated as separate hardware generations here unless later archival evidence proves materially different electronics.

The second major generation is Pivothead SMART, commercially represented by the SMART Eyewear Architect Edition. It retained the camera-first concept but added a touch/swipe interface, modular accessories, wireless/live-streaming capability, and a more explicit application/developer story.

## Generation 1 — Pivothead video-recording eyewear

Commercial evidence is strong. B&H records sold Pivothead 1080p Video Recording Sunglasses with manufacturer part numbers, bundled lenses/accessories, warranty, and detailed specifications. The documented core included an 8 MP Sony CMOS sensor, 1080p30 and 720p60 recording, 8 GB internal flash, microphone, micro-USB, 440 mAh battery, and desktop/mobile configuration. Contemporary law-enforcement coverage also records a $349 retail price and roughly 75 g frame weight.

These are camera glasses rather than display glasses: there is no near-eye HUD. Their importance is historical because they are an early example of socially wearable, centered point-of-view capture built directly into eyewear.

### Common-ruler rationale

- **Hardware 5.5:** strong embedded imaging for the 2012–2014 period, but no display, limited compute, 8 GB fixed storage, and older battery/connectivity constraints.
- **Wearability 7.0:** roughly 75 g and visibly sporty, but fundamentally recognizable as sunglasses rather than a headset.
- **Visual AI N/A:** face-tracking-style camera features do not establish a modern visual-AI assistant.
- **Software 5.0:** desktop configuration plus optional mobile workflow were useful but narrow and camera-centric.
- **Openness 3.5:** ordinary USB access and configurable settings help, but no evidence package establishes open firmware/hardware.
- **Owner Control 7.5:** local capture, local storage, and direct USB file access materially favor the owner over a cloud-bound appliance.
- **Cloud Independence 9.0:** core photo/video capture and retrieval do not inherently require a vendor cloud.
- **Hackability 4.0:** accessible files and host-side configuration provide some experimentation surface, but the embedded platform remains proprietary.
- **Display/HUD N/A:** there is no near-eye display.
- **Value 7.0:** contemporaneous ~$349 pricing bought capable POV capture in an unusually compact eyewear form.

## Generation 2 — Pivothead SMART / Architect Edition

B&H's surviving commercial listing establishes the SMART Eyewear Architect Edition as a real product rather than an announcement. It documents 1080p perspective capture, an integrated touch/swipe panel, LED and sound feedback, swappable/prescription-compatible lenses, and optional streaming accessories. Earlier launch material for the SMART line described a modular architecture and an SDK intended for developers.

The second generation therefore deserves to be preserved separately from the first generation. However, the present evidence package does not yet support a full generation-specific numeric report card across all dimensions without over-inheriting first-generation internals. The product is admitted as a distinct lineage member; unsupported fields remain ungraded pending archived manuals/SDK pages.
