# Lineage Research — ThirdEye X-Series

**Research date:** 2026-08-12

This packet applies the lineage-first protocol to ThirdEye's enterprise mixed-reality eyewear. The family evidence supports a commercial X-series centered on X2, with older X1 references and a later Alpha1 device documented by ThirdEye. Because the surviving evidence quality differs sharply by generation, scores are only assigned where the evidence is generation-specific.

## Lineage finding

### X1 Smart Glasses — documented lineage member, not yet fully graded

ThirdEye's current enterprise material still references **X1 Smart Glasses** in field-service and healthcare material, which is enough to preserve X1 as an earlier member of the X-series lineage. The surviving first-party pages available in this pass do not provide a clean product sheet, complete generation-specific specification set, or current/archived purchase route. X1 is therefore retained as a documented lineage member but is not assigned inherited X2 specifications or scores.

### X2 MR Glasses — admit and grade

X2 is the best-documented ThirdEye generation and clearly crosses the acquisition threshold. ThirdEye currently lists it for **$2,450** with an Add To Cart route. The product page identifies 1280 × 720 waveguide displays, Snapdragon XR1, 4 GB RAM, 64 GB flash, free software-developer support, VisionEye SLAM SDK support, and a standalone mixed-reality design. ThirdEye's FAQ adds built-in GPS, multiple depth sensors/cameras, prescription-frame availability, offline USB-C updating as well as OTA, a built-in 8×8 thermal sensor, and hard-hat mounting support.

ThirdEye's broader enterprise pages describe the family as Android-based and customizable, while the X2 product page documents RealEye AI, proprietary MDM, remote collaboration, workflow software, AWS-backed upload paths, and a built-in low-resolution thermal sensor. This combination gives X2 substantial local capability and developer access, but it remains a proprietary enterprise platform rather than an open-hardware/open-firmware system.

### Alpha1 MR Glasses — preserve, acquisition status not yet fully adjudicated

ThirdEye's current Trade Agreements Act statement explicitly identifies **Alpha1 MR Glasses** as a ThirdEye product, designed in the United States and assembled in the United States and Taiwan. That is strong first-party evidence that Alpha1 is a real hardware generation, not a rumor or prototype label. However, the current evidence package does not yet provide a product page, price, direct purchase route, or sufficiently complete Alpha1-specific technical sheet. Alpha1 therefore remains in the lineage record but is not silently assigned X2 characteristics or a numeric report card.

### MIDAS — adjacent form factor, not X-series smart glasses

ThirdEye also markets MIDAS, a monocular AR display integrated into protective masks. Under the wearable-HCI taxonomy, MIDAS belongs on the adjacent head-worn/protective-equipment shelf, not in the smart-glasses count and not as a continuation of the X-series eyewear lineage.

### Common-ruler rationale

- **Hardware 8.0:** Snapdragon XR1, 4 GB RAM, 64 GB flash, multiple depth/camera sensors, GPS, built-in thermal sensing, USB-C expansion and standalone compute make X2 a strong enterprise AR platform, though not modern-best hardware.
- **Wearability 4.0:** roughly 11 ounces and head-strap/hard-hat-oriented ergonomics put it well outside ordinary eyewear, despite prescription-frame support and a glasses-like optical architecture.
- **Visual AI 6.5:** ThirdEye supplies object/facial-recognition, OCR, translation and related RealEye/AssistEye functions, but these are enterprise application capabilities rather than a general visual-AI assistant comparable to current consumer AI-glasses leaders.
- **Software 8.0:** RealEye AI, remote collaboration, MDM, workflow software, SLAM tooling and developer support create a broad enterprise software stack.
- **Openness 6.5:** Android foundations and SDK/developer support are meaningful, but the stack, hardware and firmware remain proprietary and do not approach the Monocle/Frame 10-level benchmark.
- **Owner Control 7.0:** Android customization, developer support, local apps, USB-C and offline update support provide substantial control, tempered by proprietary management/software layers.
- **Cloud Independence 7.5:** core standalone AR operation and offline USB-C updates reduce dependence on vendor cloud services, though RealEye/MDM/remote-assistance workflows and AWS-backed services add optional or operational cloud reliance.
- **Hackability 6.5:** Android plus SDK access and developer support create a useful experimentation surface, but firmware/hardware are not openly documented.
- **Display/HUD 8.0:** 1280 × 720 waveguide optics, depth-aware MR and SLAM are strong for enterprise eyewear, though still below the best modern optical systems in the catalog.
- **Value 5.5:** $2,450 is expensive, but the price buys a standalone enterprise MR computer, thermal sensing, developer support and a mature software stack rather than a simple display accessory.
