# Evidence-derived profile — Lenovo ThinkReality A6

## Lenovo ThinkReality A6

Lenovo's ThinkReality A6 was the company's first ThinkReality hardware product and a serious enterprise AR system rather than a glasses-shaped peripheral. The headset paired transparent binocular Lumus waveguide optics with a Snapdragon 845 Android compute box, Intel Movidius vision processing, 13 MP RGB imaging, depth sensing, full SLAM, 6DoF inside-out tracking, gesture support, voice recognition and object recognition. Lenovo specified 1080p resolution per eye, a diagonal field of view greater than 40 degrees, more than four hours of battery life, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and cellular connectivity, while the headset itself weighed under 380 grams.

That architecture made the A6 unusually capable but not especially discreet. It was an industrial AR headset intended for hands-free enterprise workflows, and its separate compute box, controller and visor-style hardware place it far from normal-eyewear wearability. In return, it offered a richer sensing and spatial-computing stack than most ordinary smart glasses of its period.

The ownership story is mixed in an interesting way. Lenovo published an Android open-source package for the A6 and supported a ThinkReality developer platform, giving developers meaningful access to the software stack. At the same time, Lenovo's enterprise workflow used the ThinkReality Cloud Portal for device registration and application/content deployment, and deeper platform documentation was access-controlled. The result is substantially more developer-friendly than a sealed consumer appliance, but not close to open-hardware systems such as Brilliant Labs Monocle or Frame.

Lenovo's launch material placed the A6 into a limited Q3 2019 release through the ThinkReality Developer Program and global enterprise accounts, and surviving support pages list regional marketing part numbers specifically used to purchase the product. That evidence is enough to treat A6 as a shipped commercial enterprise generation rather than a prototype. A defensible contemporaneous primary-source price has not yet been recovered, so Value remains ungraded until the archival record improves.
