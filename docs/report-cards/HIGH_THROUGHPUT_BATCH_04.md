**Research date:** 2026-08-12

This packet continues the tethered-XR lineage with 14 models: VITURE Luma Ultra, VITURE Beast, TCL/RayNeo NXTWEAR G, TCL NXTWEAR G+, NXTWEAR S, NXTWEAR S+, RayNeo Air, Air 2, Air 2s, Air 3s, Air 3s Pro, Rokid Air, Rokid Max, and Rokid Max 2.

## Common-ruler interpretation

These devices are principally wearable displays. Visual AI is therefore N/A unless a model has an independently documented sensing/AI stack; lack of a camera is not treated as a defect in a display product. Cloud Independence and practical Owner Control can be strong because USB-C DisplayPort hosts remain owner-selectable even where firmware and optics are proprietary. Openness/Hackability only rise when a documented SDK, spatial API, sensor access, or developer surface exists.

## Evidence notes

VITURE's current Luma family establishes the upper end of this packet: high-resolution Micro-OLED display hardware, broad host compatibility, and—on Ultra—spatial/6DoF capability that justifies a materially higher developer score than ordinary display glasses. Beast emphasizes maximum virtual-screen/display performance over discreet wearability.

TCL's NXTWEAR lineage and the RayNeo Air lineage show the maturation of the same basic architecture: host-driven USB-C video, increasingly good Micro-OLED optics, better audio and ergonomics, but little reason to award high openness scores where the glasses remain proprietary display endpoints. The architecture nevertheless earns excellent Cloud Independence because normal video display does not require a manufacturer cloud.

Rokid Air/Max/Max 2 follow the same host-driven pattern, with Max generations improving display quality, refresh behavior, ergonomics and ecosystem integration. Rokid's software/developer ecosystem is more substantial than a bare monitor, but these models are not open hardware and should not be graded as if they were standalone AR computers.
