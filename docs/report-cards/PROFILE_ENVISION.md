# Evidence-derived profiles — Envision

## Envision Glasses

Envision Glasses are a software-defined accessibility product built on Google Glass Enterprise Edition 2 rather than a unique Envision hardware platform. The underlying XR1 computer, camera, monocular display and Android base are capable but no longer cutting-edge; what distinguishes the product is Envision's mature assistive stack for reading, scene understanding, object/person finding, calling and conversational help. Importantly, a meaningful subset of those functions can operate offline, so the glasses are not reduced to inert hardware when connectivity disappears. The tradeoff is price: current software/support editions range from roughly $1,899 to $3,499 on the same core hardware.

## Ally Solos Glasses

Ally Solos Glasses represent Envision's move toward far more ordinary-looking camera/audio eyewear. Built with Solos and driven through the Ally smartphone app, they give the wearer voice-first access to reading, scene description, translation, object identification, web information and everyday assistant functions without the visible Glass-style monocular display. That greatly improves wearability and price, but it also shifts compute and service dependence outward to the phone and AI stack, making cloud independence and owner control weaker than on the older Envision Glasses.
