# Evidence-derived profiles — NuEyes

## NuEyes e2+

The e2+ represents NuEyes' self-contained assistive branch: a heavy but independent headset with its own Snapdragon-class compute, camera, storage, magnification and OCR/text-to-speech. Its strengths are local operation and purpose-built accessibility; its weakness is wearability, because at 268 g it behaves much more like a compact headset than ordinary glasses.

## NuEyes Pro 3

Pro 3 was the important pivot from bulky assistive headsets toward a lighter AR-glasses architecture. NuEyes documented 4K optics, an 88 g form, camera-rich controller configurations and a real SDK with SLAM plus object, speech and gesture recognition. That makes it one of the more developer-oriented accessibility/enterprise products of its era, though it remains proprietary hardware rather than an open platform.

## NuEyes Pro 3e

Pro 3e strips the concept toward a simple host-driven display peripheral. Its 68 g glasses connect over USB-C DisplayPort and inherit compute, apps and network behavior from the attached phone/tablet/computer. That makes the glasses themselves extremely cloud-independent and gives owners broad software choice, even though the device has little native compute or visual AI.

## NuEyes Pro 4

Pro 4 brings camera sensing back into the lightweight tethered branch while retaining USB-C host dependence. Its binocular OLED display, 12 MP camera and compact foldable form support low-vision, medical and enterprise use without turning the glasses into a sealed standalone appliance. It is therefore a useful middle ground between pure display glasses and self-contained assistive computers.
