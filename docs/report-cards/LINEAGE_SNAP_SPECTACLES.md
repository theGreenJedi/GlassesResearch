# Snap Spectacles lineage — evidence and Report Cards

This packet grades generations only from their own evidence. The 2021 fourth-generation creator hardware is not assigned fifth-generation Snap OS features.

## GLS-0067 — Snap Spectacles (2021, 4th Gen)

**Evidence lane:** vendor-primary documentation. No GlassesResearch hands-on claims are made.

Fourth-generation Spectacles were Snap's first true display AR glasses. Dual waveguides, two RGB cameras, spatial tracking, microphones, speakers and Snapdragon XR1 compute enabled world-anchored Lenses built in Lens Studio. The technical ambition was real; so were the constraints: 134 g, roughly 30 minutes per charge and creator-program distribution rather than ordinary ownership.

Lens Studio gave selected creators a serious supported authoring and wireless deployment workflow. That earns meaningful Software and Openness credit, but it is narrower than general application sideloading or owner-modifiable firmware. The device belongs high in the historical AR-development ledger while remaining dependent on Snap's platform and access model.

| Dimension | Score | Judgment |
|---|---:|---|
| Hardware | 7.5 | XR1 compute, cameras, spatial tracking, audio and binocular waveguides were ambitious for 2021, but endurance sharply limited the package. |
| Wearability | 5.0 | A 134 g frame and approximately 30-minute battery made this experimental creator hardware rather than practical everyday eyewear. |
| Visual AI | 7.5 | Dual cameras, Scan and hand/marker/surface tracking supported meaningful understanding and anchoring of the surrounding world. |
| Software | 8.0 | Lens Studio provided a mature visual-development workflow, wireless deployment and a broad Lens ecosystem. |
| Display / HUD | 7.5 | Dual 3D waveguides, 26.3-degree FOV, high dynamic brightness and low latency delivered real binocular AR within a limited field. |
| Openness | 7.0 | Selected creators received substantial supported authoring tools, but access and deployment remained controlled by Snap's platform. |
| Owner Control | 5.5 | Creators controlled Lens content, not the underlying operating system, firmware or platform endpoints. |
| Cloud Independence | 5.0 | On-device rendering existed, but distribution, development and many intelligent experiences remained materially tied to Snap's ecosystem. |
| Hackability | 5.0 | Lens Studio was powerful within its intended boundary; no comparable public low-level firmware or general sideloading path is documented. |
| Value | N/A | Spectacles were offered to selected creators and were not sold as a retail product. |
