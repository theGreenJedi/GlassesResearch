# Lineage Research — Optinvent ORA

**Research date:** 2026-08-12

This packet applies the lineage-first protocol to the Optinvent ORA family before deciding which members belong in the smart-glasses purchasing ledger and before assigning report-card scores.

## Lineage map

The documented family is ORA-1 → ORA-2, with ORA-X reusing the same core display/software ideas in a headphone form factor. ORA-EYE is a software/service solution built around ORA-2, not a separate glasses model. ORA-H was explicitly shown by Optinvent as an early smart-helmet prototype and therefore is not a purchasable smart-glasses row.

### ORA-1 — qualifies as a purchasable developer smart-glasses model

Optinvent launched ORA-1 through Kickstarter and described it as a developer-friendly smart-glasses platform capable of running Android applications natively, with selected pledges including the Wikitude AR Browser and SDK. Optinvent later stated that it had designed, built and delivered ORA-1 hardware, and its company history says ORA-1 and ORA-2 were being produced to meet customer demand. This clears the acquisition threshold.

The surviving first-party record is much stronger on platform identity and developer intent than on complete generation-specific specifications, so this card intentionally avoids inheriting ORA-2 hardware details into ORA-1.

### ORA-2 — qualifies as a purchasable professional smart-glasses model

ORA-2 is the clearest commercial member of the lineage. Optinvent's current product and shop pages document a 699 EUR ex-VAT sale price, Android AOSP 4.4.2, a dual-core processor/GPU, 5 MP autofocus camera, microphone/audio, 9-axis sensing, GPS, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth LE, trackpad, 1200 mAh battery, roughly five hours display-on time, about 90 g total mass, and Optinvent's adjustable Flip-Vu transparent display. The product page explicitly includes an Android SDK/manual in the box.

The current page is marked out of stock and also warns that ORA-2 was not certified as a general-consumer product. That is consistent with its professional/developer positioning rather than evidence against its historical acquisition route.

### ORA-X — lineage member, but not a smart-glasses ledger row

ORA-X is a legitimate Optinvent product and remains listed at 499 EUR ex-VAT with a pre-order route. It runs Android, exposes a developer path, and uses a flip-down transparent display derived from ORA-1 technology. However, Optinvent itself positions ORA-X as over-ear smart AR headphones. Under the current catalog boundary, that makes it adjacent wearable-display hardware rather than glasses. It should be preserved in lineage/history notes but not silently counted as a smart-glasses model.

### ORA-EYE — not a hardware generation

Optinvent identifies ORA-EYE as a remote-maintenance/assistance application and service package that works with ORA-2. It is therefore software/solution packaging, not a distinct glasses model.

### ORA-H — prototype only

Optinvent's own announcement calls ORA-H an early prototype smart AR helmet. It does not meet the purchasable smart-glasses threshold and remains outside the ledger.

### ORA-1 rationale

- **Hardware — Not yet graded:** surviving first-party evidence establishes a real Android smart-glasses platform but is insufficient for a defensible generation-specific hardware score without archival recovery.
- **Wearability 3.5:** surviving images and positioning show visibly bulky developer AR eyewear, well below ordinary-glasses wearability.
- **Visual AI N/A:** the product is a programmable AR display/computing platform; current evidence does not establish a native visual-AI system comparable to the catalog's visual-AI benchmark.
- **Software 7.0:** native Android applications, developer orientation, Wikitude integration and dedicated support make ORA-1 materially more programmable than appliance-style glasses.
- **Openness 6.5:** Android plus SDK access is meaningful, but there is no evidence of open schematics, open firmware, or Monocle/Frame-class low-level access.
- **Owner Control 7.5:** owner-written Android applications and a general-purpose computing model provide substantial freedom from a fixed vendor-assistant workflow.
- **Cloud Independence 9.0:** local Android execution does not inherently require Optinvent cloud services.
- **Hackability 6.5:** Android and SDK access create a strong experimentation surface, but proprietary hardware/firmware cap the score.
- **Display/HUD 7.0:** the ORA optical concept was a serious see-through AR implementation, but incomplete surviving first-party generation-specific data prevents a higher score.
- **Value 5.5:** Kickstarter/developer acquisition made advanced AR hardware obtainable, but incomplete contemporaneous pricing/spec evidence keeps this near the middle of the catalog-wide ruler.

### ORA-2 rationale

- **Hardware 7.5:** standalone Android compute, 5 MP autofocus camera, inertial sensors, GPS, audio, Wi-Fi/BLE and a large 1200 mAh battery make ORA-2 a capable all-in-one professional platform, offset by age, bulk and modest compute.
- **Wearability 4.5:** roughly 90 g and safety-eyewear styling are practical for professional tasks but far from everyday ordinary-eyewear comfort.
- **Visual AI N/A:** camera and sensors support computer-vision applications, but Optinvent does not document a native visual-AI assistant/understanding system.
- **Software 7.5:** Android AOSP, preloaded general applications, an SDK and direct application development create a broad software surface.
- **Openness 6.5:** developer access is real and documented, but firmware, schematics and low-level hardware internals remain proprietary.
- **Owner Control 8.0:** standalone Android application execution, sideload/development pathways and local device I/O provide strong owner/developer control relative to closed consumer eyewear.
- **Cloud Independence 9.0:** core applications and device operation can run locally; vendor cloud is not required for the fundamental platform.
- **Hackability 7.0:** Android, SDK access and developer tooling make ORA-2 unusually experiment-friendly for its era, though it remains proprietary hardware.
- **Display/HUD 8.0:** >3000 cd/m2 brightness, Flip-Vu positioning and a full-color transparent display were strong professional AR capabilities, but the optics/form factor remain below the modern best-in-catalog ceiling.
- **Value 6.5:** 699 EUR ex-VAT bought a complete standalone professional AR computer with SDK access; good capability-per-cost, but not benchmark value.
