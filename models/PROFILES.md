# Smart-Glasses Model Profiles

A model entry is not complete merely because it appears in a catalog. Each GlassesResearch profile should explain, in ordinary language, **what the glasses really are, what is interesting about them, where they are strong, and what tradeoffs matter**. The structured Report Card remains useful underneath; this page is the human-readable layer.

Profiles are published only when the available evidence supports something more useful than generic product description. Missing profiles are research work to be done, not invitations to manufacture filler.

## GLS-0001 — Ray-Ban Stories

Ray-Ban Stories is best understood as the proof that camera-and-audio electronics could disappear into recognizable everyday eyewear, not as a modern AI-glasses platform. Meta and EssilorLuxottica squeezed dual 5 MP cameras, open-ear speakers, three microphones, touch controls, a battery and a Snapdragon processor into familiar Ray-Ban frames while retaining prescription and conventional lens options. Its intelligence layer was comparatively modest and its media workflow depended heavily on the Facebook View phone app, but the product established a pattern the category still follows: **wearability first, phone-assisted computing second, no HUD at all**. Historically, its most important contribution may be that it made smart glasses look less like a headset and more like something a person might actually wear all day.

Source: [Meta — Introducing Ray-Ban Stories](https://about.fb.com/news/2021/09/introducing-ray-ban-stories-smart-glasses/)

## GLS-0002 — Ray-Ban Meta (Gen 1)

The 2023 Ray-Ban Meta generation turned the Stories experiment into a much more capable everyday-computing product. Meta moved to a single 12 MP ultrawide camera, improved the speakers and microphone array, enabled livestreaming, and made Meta AI a native voice experience; later software added Meta AI with Vision, allowing the system to answer questions about what the wearer was looking at. That makes Gen 1 much more consequential than a simple camera upgrade: it is where the Ray-Ban line crossed from **capture glasses into perception-and-assistant glasses**. Its major tradeoff is architectural rather than cosmetic—the hardware is extremely wearable, but much of the intelligence, account model and evolving feature set remains tied to Meta's software and cloud ecosystem.

Sources: [Meta — Introducing the New Ray-Ban Meta Smart Glasses](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/09/new-ray-ban-meta-smart-glasses/), [Meta — Meta AI with Vision update](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/new-ray-ban-meta-smart-glasses-styles-and-meta-ai-updates/)

## GLS-0003 — Ray-Ban Meta (Gen 2)

Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 is an evolutionary product in the best sense: Meta kept the form factor people were already willing to wear and attacked two practical limitations—battery life and capture quality. The 2025 generation reaches up to eight hours of typical use and records 3K video, while retaining the camera, open-ear audio and multimodal Meta AI experience that defined the previous generation. That combination makes it one of the strongest benchmarks for **mainstream wearability plus useful visual AI**, but not for owner control: its usefulness grows through Meta's app, models and service integrations rather than through an open SDK or replaceable intelligence layer. It is therefore an excellent example of how a product can score very highly as consumer hardware while remaining much less compelling as an owner-controlled computing platform.

Source: [Meta — Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/ray-ban-meta-gen-2-better-battery-life-video-capture/)

## GLS-0004 — Meta Ray-Ban Display

Meta Ray-Ban Display is where Meta's consumer glasses stop being screen-free and begin edging toward true visual computing. The glasses add a full-color, high-resolution display and ship with the Meta Neural Band, an EMG wristband that turns subtle muscle signals into interface commands. That matters more than simply adding a HUD: it creates a new interaction model in which information can appear privately in the wearer's view without requiring constant voice commands or reaching for a phone. The result is more capable than camera/audio Ray-Bans but also more system-dependent—the value comes from the tightly integrated glasses, neural input device, Meta AI software and service stack working together. It is a major capability step, but also a useful test of whether **richer interfaces inevitably mean deeper ecosystem dependence**.

Source: [Meta — Meta Ray-Ban Display](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/meta-ray-ban-display-ai-glasses-emg-wristband/)

## GLS-0005 — Ray-Ban Meta Blayzer Optics (Gen 2)

Blayzer Optics is significant because Meta stopped treating prescription support as an accessory to sunglasses and designed a Gen 2 frame specifically around people who rely on corrective eyewear all day. The rectangular Blayzer uses overextension hinges, interchangeable nose pads and optician-adjustable temple tips, and Meta says the optical line supports nearly all prescriptions. Underneath, it remains part of the Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 AI platform, so the important change is not a new intelligence architecture but **better physical ownership of the form factor**: fit, lens compatibility and day-long comfort become primary engineering requirements rather than secondary options. For anyone who actually needs glasses to see, that can matter more than another incremental camera feature.

Source: [Meta — First AI Glasses Built for Prescriptions](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/meta-ai-glasses-built-for-prescriptions/)

## GLS-0006 — Ray-Ban Meta Scriber Optics (Gen 2)

Scriber Optics shares the prescription-first engineering of Blayzer but packages it in a rounder frame, giving the optical Gen 2 line a second everyday style rather than forcing prescription users into a single industrial-looking solution. Its overextension hinges, replaceable nose pads and adjustable temple tips reinforce the same idea: smart glasses meant to replace ordinary eyewear must be fitted like ordinary eyewear. Technically it inherits the strengths and constraints of the Gen 2 Meta platform—strong capture, audio and visual AI, paired with substantial dependence on Meta's application and cloud services. The interesting part is therefore not raw capability but **normalization**: the computer is being designed around the optician's chair as much as around the electronics bench.

Source: [Meta — First AI Glasses Built for Prescriptions](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/meta-ai-glasses-built-for-prescriptions/)

## GLS-0007 — Oakley Meta HSTN

Oakley Meta HSTN takes the Ray-Ban Meta idea and shifts the optimization target from ordinary fashion eyewear toward active use. It combines a 12 MP camera, open-ear audio, Meta AI, 3K capture, IPX4 water resistance and up to eight hours of typical battery life in an Oakley frame, with PRIZM lens options on some models. The result is not merely a rebadge: its styling, durability assumptions and audience make it a better fit for people who want camera-and-AI glasses during outdoor activity than for someone prioritizing discretion in an office. Its central tradeoff remains familiar across Meta's line—**excellent integration and mature AI functionality, but comparatively little freedom to replace the software stack or intelligence provider**.

Source: [Meta — Introducing Oakley Meta HSTN](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/06/introducing-oakley-meta-glasses-a-new-category-of-performance-ai-glasses/)

## GLS-0008 — Oakley Meta Vanguard

Oakley Meta Vanguard pushes the performance concept much farther than HSTN. It is a wraparound sports device with IP67 protection, a centered 12 MP wide-angle camera capable of 3K capture, stronger open-ear speakers, a wind-optimized five-microphone array and up to nine hours of battery life. More interestingly, it connects the glasses to Garmin, Strava and health-data ecosystems so Meta AI can surface training metrics and even trigger activity-aware capture. That makes Vanguard one of the clearest examples of smart glasses becoming **situational equipment rather than generic eyewear**: the frame, audio, camera, sensors and integrations are designed around running, cycling and training. It gives up some normal-eyeglasses discretion in exchange for a much more purposeful athletic computer.

Source: [Meta — Oakley Meta Vanguard](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/oakley-meta-vanguard-performance-ai-glasses-sports/)

## Coverage rule

The target is one useful profile for every canonical GLS identifier. When the evidence is too thin to write one responsibly, the next action is investigation: verify the model, improve or correct the underlying record, and only then publish the paragraph.
