# Rokid AR Joy 2 — active system investigation

Rokid AR Joy 2 is a current commercial entertainment package built around **Rokid Max 2 glasses + the original Rokid Station**.

**Technology lineage:** [Rokid](../../lineages/ROKID.md)  
**Identity treatment:** product/system bundle; does **not** create another smart-glasses identity beyond Max 2 (`GLS-0094`)  
**Evidence state:** current first-party manufacturer documentation; hands-on verification pending

## Current first-party definition

Rokid's AR-series comparison, checked 2026-09-05, identifies AR Joy 2 as:

- Max 2 glasses plus the **original Rokid Station**;
- a single-screen entertainment experience rather than the Station 2 multi-window 3DoF workspace;
- original Station with an Android TV-style system;
- 2 GB RAM and 32 GB storage in the original Station;
- Wi-Fi 5 and Bluetooth 5.0 in the original Station;
- USB, Micro HDMI, AirPlay, Miracast, and DLNA support;
- 5000 mAh claimed Station battery capacity and approximately five-hour claimed runtime;
- remote-control interaction and Bluetooth-audio support.

## Identity and contradiction boundary

AR Joy 2 must stay separate from **AR Spatial** at the system level because the included compute host is different. AR Spatial uses Station 2 and YodaOS-Master; AR Joy 2 uses the original Rokid Station.

Rokid's individual AR Joy 2 storefront page currently contains Station 2-like copy advertising 8 GB RAM, 128 GB storage, Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.2, 18 W charging, and YodaOS-Master. That conflicts with Rokid's own AR-series comparison identifying Joy 2 as the original-Station bundle. GlassesResearch preserves this as a manufacturer-source contradiction pending independent resolution.

## Investigation queue

1. Confirm the exact Station hardware revision included in current AR Joy 2 retail packages.
2. Record model numbers, firmware versions, storage, radios, ports, and included accessories from packaging/manuals.
3. Determine whether current shipments ever substitute Station 2 or another revision under the Joy 2 name.
4. Test local/offline media use, casting protocols, account requirements, app installation, firmware updates, and recovery behavior.
5. Compare original Station service survival and owner control against Station 2/YodaOS-Master.
6. Verify Max 2 behavior when detached from the Station and connected directly to generic DisplayPort hosts.
7. Preserve pricing and bundle-composition changes over time without counting them as new glasses models unless the eyewear hardware actually changes.
