# Evidence-Derived Profiles — High-Throughput Batch 05

These paragraphs are downstream of `docs/report-cards/HIGH_THROUGHPUT_BATCH_05.md`. They preserve the report-card-first rule: evidence package first, fixed-ruler scoring second, public prose third.

## GLS-0095 — Vuzix M100

Vuzix M100 belongs to the early enterprise monocular era: a head-worn Android computer that put a camera, small color display and application platform on the wearer rather than merely mirroring a phone. Its importance is less its now-dated hardware than the ownership model it helped establish. Compared with locked consumer eyewear, installable applications and Android-style development gave operators meaningful control, while its bulky monocular form and early-generation display keep both wearability and visual output modest by current catalog standards.

## GLS-0096 — Vuzix M300

M300 pushed Vuzix's enterprise lineage toward a more practical industrial computer. The Android 6.0.1 platform exposes standard camera, sensor, Bluetooth/BLE and database APIs while Vuzix supplies device-specific speech and barcode interfaces. That makes it substantially more developer-accessible than most consumer smart glasses without making it open hardware: owners can deploy and repurpose software, but firmware, schematics and low-level internals remain vendor-controlled.

## GLS-0097 — Vuzix M300XL

M300XL is an evolutionary M300 rather than a separate software architecture. Vuzix explicitly documents the two as equivalent from a developer perspective; the XL changes the battery connection to standard Micro-B and improves camera behavior. Its enduring research value is therefore lineage evidence: relatively small hardware revisions can improve field maintainability while preserving the same Android application surface and owner-control model.

## GLS-0098 — Vuzix M400

M400 is a much more capable wearable computer than the earlier M-series devices. Qualcomm XR1 compute, 6 GB RAM, 64 GB storage, a 12.8 MP/4K camera, sensors, triple microphones, touch, physical controls and voice interaction give it a serious local sensing and application platform. The 640×360 occluded OLED is comparatively narrow as a modern HUD, but Android development, APK installation and standard sensor/camera APIs make M400 unusually controllable for proprietary enterprise eyewear. Its strongest characteristic is not openness in the Monocle/Frame sense; it is the ability to run owner-selected software without making a vendor cloud the center of the device.

## GLS-0099 — Vuzix M4000

M4000 shares the M400's XR1-class compute, memory, camera and Android development model but changes the visual experience materially with an 854×480 see-through waveguide. That makes it the more AR-like member of the pair: the wearer can retain direct sight of the environment while receiving digital information. The same distinction in ownership applies—strong application-level control and cloud independence, but proprietary firmware and hardware keep it below genuinely open developer platforms.

## GLS-0100 — Vuzix LX1

LX1 is unapologetically an industrial tool. Its Android 15 platform, 7000 mAh long-shift battery, rugged/freezer-oriented construction and NFC pairing target warehouse workflows rather than social discretion. That trade is visible in the report card: strong hardware, software and owner application control coexist with low ordinary-eyewear wearability. LX1 is useful precisely because the catalog does not grade enterprise ergonomics on a separate curve—the score shows what capability was gained and what was sacrificed.

## GLS-0121 — Vuzix Shield

Shield is one of Vuzix's most ambitious standalone visual systems, combining Snapdragon XR1 compute with binocular full-color microLED waveguides, stereo HD cameras, voice/touch interaction and prescription-ready safety-glasses framing. The binocular see-through display is a substantial step beyond the company's monocular M-series optics, while Android-oriented development preserves meaningful application control. Shield therefore scores strongly in hardware, visual sensing and HUD capability, but its proprietary internals still distinguish it sharply from the catalog's open-hardware benchmarks.

## GLS-0101 — RealWear HMT-1

RealWear HMT-1 represents a different branch of enterprise eyewear: rugged, voice-first and deliberately optimized for hands-free field work rather than conventional-glasses appearance. Its 854×480 monocular display and Android platform supported practical local applications, and later firmware brought the device to Android 10. RealWear has now ended support at firmware 12.6, making HMT-1 both a legacy product and a useful case study in how cloud-independent local functions can outlive a vendor's active support window.

## GLS-0102 — RealWear HMT-1Z1

HMT-1Z1 adapts the HMT architecture for intrinsically safe environments. That is a meaningful hardware achievement for hazardous industrial work, but the additional rugged specialization does not become a wearability bonus on the common catalog ruler. Like HMT-1, it is now end-of-support; its value to the research record lies in showing how smart-eyewear design changes when safety certification and field reliability outrank size, style and social discretion.

## GLS-0103 — RealWear Navigator 500

Navigator 500 modernized RealWear's voice-first field-computing platform with a modular camera and swappable battery while retaining the 854×480 monocular display format. It remains a supported enterprise device, and RealWear's long support horizon materially strengthens its software score relative to discontinued HMT hardware. The architecture offers meaningful owner application deployment, but not the firmware, schematic or low-level freedom required for top-tier openness.

## GLS-0104 — RealWear Navigator 520

Navigator 520's clearest generational improvement is visual: its 0.35-inch 1280×720 HD display substantially improves on the 854×480 panel used by Navigator 500 and the HMT family. It keeps the modular, rugged, hands-free RealWear approach while remaining in the company's supported-device program. For field work that combination is compelling, though its head-mounted boom architecture remains far from ordinary-eyewear wearability.

## GLS-0105 — RealWear Navigator Z1

Navigator Z1 combines the newer 1280×720 RealWear display platform with intrinsically safe construction for hazardous environments. It is therefore the mature successor to HMT-1Z1 rather than merely a cosmetic variant of Navigator 520. Its hardware and software are strong on the catalog-wide ruler, particularly for a supported rugged computer, while proprietary platform internals and a specialized head-worn form prevent enterprise usefulness from being mistaken for benchmark openness or wearability.
