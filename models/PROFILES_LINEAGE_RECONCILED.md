# Lineage-Reconciled Smart-Glasses Profiles

These profiles bind the 2026-08-12 lineage-first evidence packets to the canonical `GLS-####` IDs. They are intentionally evidence-derived: when a generation is known to have existed and been obtainable but generation-specific internals remain incomplete, the paragraph says so rather than inheriting specifications from a neighboring model.

## GLS-0122 — ODG R-7

ODG's **R-7** was a self-contained enterprise AR computer worn as glasses, shipping by late 2015 at an enterprise-class price around $2,750. Android/ReticleOS, binocular see-through optics, onboard cameras and sensors, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth and a real application-development path made it unusually owner-programmable for its era. Its weakness was the physical reality of early standalone AR: bulky industrial eyewear, limited modern support and proprietary hardware. Core applications could run locally, so cloud independence remains one of its strongest historical traits. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_ODG_R_SERIES.md)

## GLS-0123 — Recon Jet

**Recon Jet** turned a small near-eye HUD, camera, GPS/sensors and Android-derived software into sport eyewear that actually reached retail in 2015. Recon sold it directly and through retailers including Amazon, and exposed an SDK rather than treating the glasses as a sealed appliance. The later shutdown of Recon Engage is the cautionary part of the story: useful local hardware survived, but account/server dependencies degraded ownership after Intel ended the service. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_RECON_JET.md)

## GLS-0124 — Recon / Intel Jet Pro

**Jet Pro** is a documented successor in the same Recon/Intel commercial family, aimed more explicitly at professional use. Intel's surviving support material establishes it as a real customer product, but the current evidence package does not justify copying original Jet specifications into Pro field by field. It therefore remains a canonical generation with conservative generation-specific scoring until deeper archival material is recovered. [Lineage evidence](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_RECON_JET.md)

## GLS-0125 — Recon / Intel Jet Pro+

**Jet Pro+** is likewise preserved as a distinct sold Recon customer product rather than collapsed into the original Jet. The lineage evidence proves the generation existed in the commercial/support chain, while detailed model-specific hardware evidence is thinner than for Jet. GlassesResearch therefore records the product without manufacturing a specification sheet from family resemblance. [Lineage evidence](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_RECON_JET.md)

## GLS-0126 — Optinvent ORA-1

**ORA-1** was Optinvent's first delivered Android smart-glasses developer platform. It matters less as a polished consumer product than as evidence that Optinvent crossed from optical prototypes into obtainable programmable eyewear. Surviving first-party material supports the Android/developer character and delivery history, but not enough first-generation hardware detail for a fully populated hardware score; those fields remain intentionally ungraded rather than borrowed from ORA-2. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_OPTINVENT_ORA.md)

## GLS-0127 — Optinvent ORA-2

**ORA-2** is the much better documented Optinvent generation: Android AOSP, dual-core compute, a 5 MP autofocus camera, 9-axis sensing, GPS, Wi-Fi/BLE, a 1200 mAh battery, roughly 90 g mass and Flip-Vu transparent display optics, plus an Android SDK. Optinvent's own store documented a €699 ex-VAT purchase route. It is bulky by ordinary-eyewear standards but unusually legible as a developer-owned standalone computer rather than a cloud-bound accessory. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_OPTINVENT_ORA.md)

## GLS-0128 — Toshiba / Dynabook dynaEdge AR100 + DE-100

The **dynaEdge AR100** is best understood as one eyewear-compute system: a 47 g monocular HMD paired over USB-C with the DE-100 Windows 10 Pro mini PC. Toshiba/Dynabook documented the camera, display, controls, sensors, prescription-compatible mounting options and IP63 protection, along with direct developer-kit procurement. Because the compute side is a general Windows PC and a reference SDK existed, owner control is unusually strong for enterprise glasses even though the HMD itself remains proprietary. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_TOSHIBA_DYNAEDGE_AR100.md)

## GLS-0129 — DAQRI Smart Glasses

**DAQRI Smart Glasses** were a real $4,995 enterprise AR product rather than a concept sibling of the better-known Smart Helmet. DAQRI documented worldwide shipment beginning in November 2017, a 44-degree stereoscopic display, depth/tracking cameras, wireless connectivity and spatial-mapping capability. They belong in the smart-glasses ledger; the Smart Helmet and Smart HUD are retained separately because form factor matters. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_DAQRI_PROFESSIONAL_AR.md)

## GLS-0130 — Lenovo ThinkReality A6

**ThinkReality A6** was Lenovo's first major enterprise AR eyewear generation before the A3 family. Snapdragon 845 compute, an Intel Movidius VPU, Lumus binocular waveguides, 1080p-per-eye imagery, cameras/depth sensing and full spatial tracking made it a substantial standalone AR system. Lenovo also published Android open-source material, but enterprise deployment could involve ThinkReality cloud management, so it is meaningfully developer-friendly without approaching the catalog's fully open benchmark devices. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_LENOVO_THINKREALITY_A6.md)

## GLS-0131 — ThirdEye X1 Smart Glasses

**ThirdEye X1** is preserved as the first commercial branch of ThirdEye's enterprise mixed-reality eyewear family. Current and archival ThirdEye material establishes the product lineage, but the strongest detailed evidence now available belongs to X2. GlassesResearch therefore recognizes X1 as a canonical sold generation while leaving unsupported internals ungraded rather than reverse-projecting X2's specification sheet into it. [Lineage evidence](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_THIRDEYE_X_SERIES.md)

## GLS-0132 — ThirdEye X2 MR Glasses

**ThirdEye X2 MR Glasses** are the best documented member of the family and remain directly orderable in ThirdEye's enterprise channel. Snapdragon XR1 compute, 4 GB RAM, 64 GB storage, 1280×720 waveguide optics, SLAM/depth/camera sensing, GPS, USB-C and built-in thermal sensing give X2 an unusually broad industrial sensor package. Developer support exists, but the platform remains proprietary enough that openness and owner control do not reach the open-hardware leaders. [Chapter](ThirdEyeXSeries/README.md) · [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_THIRDEYE_X_SERIES.md)

## GLS-0133 — ThirdEye Alpha1 MR Glasses

**Alpha1 MR Glasses** are a documented later ThirdEye generation, not a cosmetic X2 rename. ThirdEye's own compliance/product material is enough to preserve Alpha1 in the commercial lineage, but the current evidence package is intentionally conservative about generation-specific internals. As with X1, unsupported X2 details are not inherited merely because the products share a maker and market. [Chapter](ThirdEyeXSeries/README.md) · [Lineage evidence](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_THIRDEYE_X_SERIES.md)

## GLS-0134 — Pivothead Camera Glasses, first generation

Pivothead's first consumer camera-eyewear generation put an 8 MP Sony CMOS sensor, 1080p video, microphone, local storage and direct USB file access into glasses sold in style names such as Recon, Aurora, Durango and Moab. The evidence supports those names primarily as frame/style variants around a shared electronics generation, so the catalog counts one hardware row rather than multiplying color and shape choices. The architecture is historically notable for strong local ownership: recording and file access did not require a vendor cloud. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_PIVOTHEAD.md)

## GLS-0135 — Pivothead SMART / Architect Edition

**Pivothead SMART / Architect Edition** represents the second major Pivothead generation, adding a more connected, modular and developer-oriented direction with touch/swipe control and live-streaming ambitions. Contemporary material supports a genuine second product generation and developer program, but the archival record is not complete enough to copy every first-generation internal specification forward. Its canonical row therefore distinguishes what is known from what remains to be recovered. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_PIVOTHEAD.md)

## GLS-0136 — Mutrics M1

**Mutrics M1** belongs to the audio-glasses branch of the smart-eyewear family: ordinary-looking frames with open-ear audio and connected controls rather than a camera or HUD. Surviving commercial/distributor and design records establish it as a real consumer product. Because no substantial public SDK, firmware source or low-level development interface has surfaced, its strongest traits are wearability and simple cloud-independent Bluetooth use rather than openness or hackability. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_MUTRICS.md)

## GLS-0137 — Chamelo Dusk Classic

**Chamelo Dusk Classic** uses electronically adjustable tint—optionally paired with open-ear audio—inside conventional-looking sunglasses. It is smart eyewear without being AR: Visual AI and Display/HUD are correctly `N/A`. The core lens state works as a local owner-controlled function and the glasses remain highly wearable, while the closed electronics and lack of a meaningful public SDK keep openness and hackability low. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_CHAMELO.md)

## GLS-0138 — Chamelo Music Shield Gen 2

**Music Shield Gen 2** combines fast electronic tint adjustment with open-ear audio in a sport-shield frame. Chamelo documents a second-generation hardware revision, around 51 g mass, long tint-control battery life, approximately six hours of audio use, IPX4 protection and rapid tint transitions. Because tint operation is fundamentally local rather than service-dependent, cloud independence and owner control are strong even though the platform is not open to developers. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_CHAMELO.md)

## GLS-0139 — Chamelo Aura

**Chamelo Aura** uses electronically controlled Prismatic lenses to switch among color states, again making it smart eyewear without a HUD. At roughly ordinary-sunglasses weight and with prescription-ready configurations, wearability is a major strength. It is highly cloud-independent for its core lens function, but there is no evidence of an SDK or owner-replaceable firmware/AI stack; this is a polished appliance rather than an open platform. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_CHAMELO.md)

## GLS-0140 — NuEyes e2+

**NuEyes e2+** is a self-contained assistive headset-style pair of smart glasses with Snapdragon 835 compute, 64 GB storage, a wide display, 16 MP autofocus camera, OCR/text-to-speech and magnification functions. It can operate without a phone, which helps owner control and cloud independence, but at roughly 268 g it is much closer to assistive head-worn computing than discreet everyday eyewear. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_NUEYES.md)

## GLS-0141 — NuEyes Pro 3

**NuEyes Pro 3** marked a major shift toward lighter AR glasses. NuEyes documented 4K display capability, roughly 51-degree FOV and an 88 g flagship configuration with 6DoF cameras, a 20 MP RGB camera and an SDK covering SLAM plus recognition functions. The family had controller, USB-C and thermal configurations; current catalog policy treats those as one generation until evidence justifies separate hardware rows. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_NUEYES.md)

## GLS-0142 — NuEyes Pro 3e

**NuEyes Pro 3e** is the simpler display-centric branch: approximately 68 g, 1080p birdbath optics, stereo audio, IMU sensing and USB-C DisplayPort input. That host-driven architecture gives it exceptional cloud independence because core display operation belongs to the owner's source device rather than a vendor service. It is less visually intelligent than Pro 3, but materially more owner-neutral as a display peripheral. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_NUEYES.md)

## GLS-0143 — NuEyes Pro 4

**NuEyes Pro 4** is the current successor in NuEyes' tethered assistive-glasses line, combining binocular 1080p OLED displays, a 12 MP camera and onboard sensors with USB-C connection to a phone, tablet or computer. It balances substantial assistive capability with a host-controlled architecture, so the hardware itself remains relatively cloud-independent even when particular low-vision software services are more vendor-specific. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_NUEYES.md)

## GLS-0144 — Envision Glasses

**Envision Glasses** pair Envision's visual-assistance software with Google Glass Enterprise Edition 2 hardware to read text, recognize surroundings and connect blind or low-vision users to richer assistance workflows. The hardware lineage is therefore already well understood, while the distinguishing value is Envision's assistive software layer. It belongs in the smart-glasses count because the sold product is fundamentally eyewear, even though its physical platform originates with Google Glass. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_ENVISION.md)

## GLS-0145 — Envision Ally Solos

**Ally Solos** moves Envision's assistive-AI branch onto Solos eyewear rather than Glass Enterprise Edition 2, creating a lighter audio/camera-oriented wearable path. The important lineage distinction is hardware, not merely an Envision subscription tier: Read/Home/Professional support packages are software/service choices, while Ally Solos is a different physical glasses platform and therefore receives its own canonical row. [Evidence and report card](../docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_ENVISION.md)
