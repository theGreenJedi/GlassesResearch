# XR Display Lineages — VITURE, TCL/RayNeo, and Rokid

These profiles are the human-readable companion to High-Throughput Report Card Batch 04.

## GLS-0081 — VITURE Luma Ultra

Luma Ultra is the point where VITURE's display-glasses lineage begins to cross back into spatial computing. Its core appeal remains the excellent host-driven Micro-OLED display architecture of the Luma family, but Ultra adds the sensing and 6DoF ambitions that ordinary video glasses lack. That is why its report card separates it sharply from simpler tethered displays: Display/HUD is near the top of the catalog, while Openness and Hackability rise because spatial capability creates a real developer surface rather than merely accepting a video signal. It still benefits from the fundamental freedom of tethered XR—the wearer chooses the phone, PC or other host—so cloud independence remains excellent even though the glasses themselves are proprietary.

## GLS-0082 — VITURE Beast

Beast is VITURE leaning into the proposition that wearable displays should first be spectacular displays. The product sacrifices some discretion and everyday-glasses feel in exchange for a larger, more ambitious virtual-screen experience. Its card therefore peaks in Hardware and Display/HUD rather than Wearability or Openness. Like the rest of the family, its strongest ownership characteristic is architectural: the useful content and applications live on an owner-selected host, so the glasses do not need a VITURE cloud to remain useful. Beast is less interesting as an independent computer than as evidence of how far a private monitor can be pushed before the physical size of the optics begins to fight the idea of ordinary eyewear.

## GLS-0083 — TCL/RayNeo NXTWEAR G

NXTWEAR G belongs to the first serious wave of consumer glasses built around a wonderfully simple idea: make the glasses a display and let a device the user already owns do the computing. That keeps cloud dependence low and gives the owner broad freedom over content, but it also limits what the glasses themselves can do. There is no reason to pretend they are visual-AI eyewear or an open development platform. Their historical importance is instead architectural. NXTWEAR G helped establish the USB-C wearable-monitor pattern that later XREAL, VITURE, RayNeo and Rokid generations would refine with better optics, ergonomics and spatial features.

## GLS-0084 — TCL NXTWEAR G+

NXTWEAR G+ is an evolutionary refinement rather than a change in philosophy. The host still supplies the applications and video, leaving the glasses to concentrate on optics, display and wearable presentation. Improvements to the display experience lift its Hardware and HUD scores over the original G, while Cloud Independence stays high for the same reason: a conventional host connection is inherently less fragile than a proprietary cloud service. What does not change is the openness ceiling. A proprietary display peripheral can give its owner tremendous freedom over what appears on screen without giving that owner meaningful control over firmware or the optical stack.

## GLS-0085 — TCL NXTWEAR S

NXTWEAR S represents the architecture becoming genuinely practical. The display-glasses concept is no longer merely a technology demonstration; lighter construction, Micro-OLED presentation and straightforward host connectivity make it plausible as a personal screen for travel, gaming and work. Its report card consequently rewards Wearability, Display and Cloud Independence, while Visual AI remains N/A and Hackability stays modest. That distinction is important: GlassesResearch does not punish a monitor for lacking a camera, but neither does it call host freedom an open hardware platform. NXTWEAR S succeeds by being a better screen on the face, not by trying to become a computer on the face.

## GLS-0086 — TCL NXTWEAR S+

NXTWEAR S+ pushes the S formula further with a stronger display experience while preserving the host-driven design. It is a useful example of why our Cloud Independence score is separate from Openness: the glasses can remain highly useful with arbitrary compatible source devices and no manufacturer server, yet their firmware and internal behavior are still largely controlled by the vendor. The result is a very good ownership proposition for someone who wants a private portable display, but a mediocre one for someone who wants to rewrite the glasses themselves. Its strongest scores therefore sit exactly where they should—Display/HUD, practical Owner Control and Cloud Independence.

## GLS-0087 — RayNeo Air

RayNeo Air carries TCL's wearable-display lineage into a more explicitly glasses-shaped consumer product. Its job is still straightforward: turn a compatible phone, computer or console into a large private screen. That simplicity gives it durable usefulness and excellent cloud independence, because the intelligence and applications are not trapped in a RayNeo service. The tradeoff is equally straightforward. Air is not a visual-AI system, not an autonomous computer and not a particularly open device. It matters because it demonstrates the virtues of architectural restraint: by doing less on the glasses, it can stay lighter, cooler and easier to understand.

## GLS-0088 — RayNeo Air 2

Air 2 is where RayNeo's display lineage becomes notably more wearable. Better optics and ergonomics improve the experience without changing the basic division of labor: the host computes, the glasses display. That means Air 2 earns stronger Hardware, Wearability and HUD scores than its predecessor while retaining the same unusually strong Cloud Independence. It also illustrates a recurring theme in this catalog: owner control does not always require root access. Choosing the host computer, operating system, applications and media source is meaningful control even when the display firmware remains closed.

## GLS-0089 — RayNeo Air 2s

Air 2s is a polish generation. Rather than chasing cameras or autonomous AI, RayNeo focuses on the things that determine whether display glasses survive actual use: image quality, audio, comfort and fit. Its report card consequently looks similar to Air 2, because the architecture is intentionally stable. That is not a weakness. Mature product families often improve by removing friction rather than adding categories of capability. Air 2s is therefore best read as a refinement of the wearable-monitor idea, with strong display utility and host freedom but little evidence for deep firmware-level openness.

## GLS-0090 — RayNeo Air 3s

Air 3s pushes RayNeo's mature display architecture toward the upper end of consumer value. The display system and ergonomics are now good enough that the glasses make sense as a recurring personal screen rather than an occasional novelty, while the host-driven design keeps them largely indifferent to which cloud or AI service the owner prefers. That gives Air 3s an unusually balanced card: strong Hardware, Wearability, Display, Owner Control, Cloud Independence and Value, without inflated Openness or Hackability scores. It is a good example of a product becoming excellent at its actual job instead of accumulating features merely to look more futuristic.

## GLS-0091 — RayNeo Air 3s Pro

Air 3s Pro takes the same lineage and spends its additional complexity on the visual experience. The Pro designation earns its keep primarily in Display/HUD and Hardware rather than in a fundamentally different software architecture. It remains a tethered display, and that means its strongest independence comes from the computer on the other end of the cable. For GlassesResearch, that is a useful counterpoint to cloud-first AI glasses: Air 3s Pro may be less autonomous, but the owner can replace the host, replace the applications and replace the online services without replacing the eyewear.

## GLS-0092 — Rokid Air

Rokid Air is another early expression of the host-driven XR display, but it arrives from a company with broader AR ambitions. The glasses themselves are still primarily a portable display rather than a standalone spatial computer, so their card rewards the visual experience and low cloud dependence while remaining conservative about openness and visual AI. Rokid's surrounding software ecosystem gives Air somewhat more software context than a completely generic monitor, but the durable value is still the standard pattern: a compatible host owns the application logic and the glasses provide the view.

## GLS-0093 — Rokid Max

Rokid Max turns the Air concept into a more mature personal-display product. Higher-end display performance, refresh behavior and ergonomics make the glasses more compelling for media and gaming, while the lack of onboard autonomous compute keeps weight, heat and cloud dependency under control. Max therefore scores strongly in Hardware, Display/HUD, Owner Control and Cloud Independence. Its modest Openness score is deliberate: being able to display almost anything from a host is not the same thing as having source code or unrestricted access to the glasses' internals.

## GLS-0094 — Rokid Max 2

Max 2 is the refinement generation of Rokid's wearable-monitor branch. The important improvements are experiential—fit, optical presentation and the accumulated lessons of previous hardware—rather than a sudden transformation into AI glasses. That gives it one of the stronger balanced cards in this part of the catalog: excellent display utility, good wearability and very high independence from vendor cloud infrastructure. Max 2's significance is almost philosophical. It shows that a smart-glasses product can remain useful precisely because it refuses to own the whole computing stack; sometimes the most owner-friendly computer on your face is the one that lets your actual computer stay in charge.
