## GLS-0019 — HUAWEI × GENTLE MONSTER Eyewear

The first HUAWEI × GENTLE MONSTER Eyewear is best understood as an early proof that connected audio could disappear inside fashion frames without turning the wearer into a headset demonstration. It had no camera and no display, so its job was narrower: open-ear audio, calls and phone-assistant access in eyewear designed first to look socially normal. That simplicity gives it surprisingly strong cloud independence—basic Bluetooth audio does not stop being useful because a vendor AI service disappears—but the same architecture offers very little owner programmability. In our Report Card it therefore lands as **highly wearable, locally useful audio eyewear with weak openness and hackability**, an important precursor to the more mature Huawei Eyewear generations that followed.

Sources: [Huawei support](https://consumer.huawei.com/en/support/), [Report Card](../docs/report-cards/HIGH_THROUGHPUT_BATCH_01.md#gls-0019--huawei--gentle-monster-eyewear)

## GLS-0024 — Xiaomi AI Glasses

Xiaomi AI Glasses are a remarkably dense screen-free AI platform for their 40 g weight. Xiaomi combines a 12 MP Sony IMX681 first-person camera, dual speakers, a five-microphone array, Snapdragon AR1 compute, a low-power Vela OS companion chip and a 263 mAh battery rated for up to 8.6 hours of use. The camera is not merely for capture: Xiaomi explicitly builds object recognition, text translation, calorie recognition, transcription, summaries, interpretation, video calls and livestreaming around it. That earns the glasses very strong Hardware and Visual AI scores, but the architecture remains much more **vendor appliance than owner-controlled computer**—there is no comparable public SDK, replaceable assistant path or firmware-level development story. Xiaomi demonstrates that lightweight consumer AI glasses can be extremely capable while still leaving the owner outside most of the software stack.

Sources: [Xiaomi — AI Glasses](https://www.mi.com/global/discover/article?id=5172), [Report Card](../docs/report-cards/HIGH_THROUGHPUT_BATCH_01.md#gls-0024--xiaomi-ai-glasses)

## GLS-0025 — HTC VIVE Eagle

VIVE Eagle is HTC's answer to the modern camera-and-audio AI-glasses formula, but with a few architectural choices that make it more interesting than a simple Meta imitation. The roughly 49 g frame carries Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1, a 12 MP ultrawide camera, 4 GB of RAM, 32 GB of local storage, four beamforming microphones, stereo open-ear speakers, Wi-Fi 6E and Bluetooth 5.3. VIVE AI can call both Google Gemini and OpenAI models, while HTC says captured user data is stored locally with AES-256 protection rather than used to train VIVE AI. That gives Eagle better practical owner control than many closed consumer rivals, but it is **not an open developer platform**: cloud AI still supplies much of the intelligence and HTC does not document a broad low-level SDK comparable with Mentra, Solos or Brilliant Labs. The result is a strong, wearable visual-AI product whose privacy and model-choice story is better than its hackability story.

Sources: [HTC — VIVE Eagle specs](https://www.vive.com/us/product/vive-eagle/spec/), [HTC launch announcement](https://www.vive.com/us/newsroom/2025-08-14/), [Report Card](../docs/report-cards/HIGH_THROUGHPUT_BATCH_01.md#gls-0025--htc-vive-eagle)
