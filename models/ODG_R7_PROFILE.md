# ODG R-7 — Evidence-Derived Profile

**Catalog status:** admission supported; historical enterprise smartglasses

ODG's R-7 belongs in the smart-glasses purchasing history because it was not merely a CES concept: the enterprise-focused device was documented as shipping from late 2015, with contemporary pricing around $2,750. It represents an important branch of the industry's evolution in which the glasses themselves were intended to be the computer. Rather than acting as a simple phone accessory or USB-C display, R-7 combined binocular see-through visual output, cameras and sensors, wireless connectivity and Android-derived software in a self-contained industrial headset.

That architecture remains the R-7's strongest ownership characteristic. Android/ODG ReticleOS and a developer-oriented application model gave organizations substantially more control over local workloads than modern consumer glasses whose useful functions terminate in a vendor assistant or cloud service. Core applications could run on the wearable without a manufacturer cloud, producing a strong Cloud Independence score. But developer access should not be confused with openness: the hardware and firmware remained proprietary, so R-7 sits well below the Brilliant Labs Monocle/Frame benchmark for Openness and Hackability.

The tradeoff was wearability. R-7 pursued capability first and ordinary-eyewear discretion second; its industrial bulk, conspicuous optics and high acquisition price make it a poor analogue for today's all-day AI glasses. Its historical importance is instead architectural: it demonstrated surprisingly early that standalone mobile compute, sensing, binocular AR and owner-deployable applications could coexist in a head-worn device. That makes R-7 useful context for modern standalone AR systems even though its optics, ergonomics and dead vendor ecosystem have been overtaken.
