# Canonical reconciliation — display-glasses gap audit

Date: 2026-09-04

This packet closes a focused gap in the purchaser-history ledger for consumer display glasses. The audit began with the CORNMI NeoVista A8 Lite Alibaba listing and then collision-checked the existing XREAL, VITURE, TCL/RayNeo, Rokid, Meizu/StarV, Lenovo, ASUS, Huawei and OPPO display branches before allocating any new identities.

The existing ledger already covers the major XREAL, VITURE, RayNeo Air, Rokid Air/Max and TCL NXTWEAR G/G+/S/S+ families. Those models are not duplicated here. The rows below are additions only where a distinct marketed hardware identity and a paid acquisition route are established.

## Identity decisions

### CORNMI NeoVista family

CORNMI presently operates a direct storefront with four separately named and separately purchasable NeoVista display products. X7 Lite is the lower-resolution 1920×1080-per-eye member. X7 is separately sold at 2560×1440 per eye. X7 Pro is separately sold and explicitly marketed with built-in Android OS, making it a materially different computing configuration rather than a cosmetic trim. A8 Lite has its own SKU and direct purchase route and is additionally offered through OEM/marketplace channels.

The manufacturer evidence is internally inconsistent enough that canonical admission must not be mistaken for spec verification. The A8 Lite storefront advertises roughly 79 g, while CORNMI's own product specification page lists 238 g. Its headline calls the display “Micro-LED” while the specification table says Micro OLED; another CORNMI article has even described a liquid-crystal waveguide. X7 Lite is similarly described as 228 g in editorial material and 238 g on the spec page, and older CORNMI material has disagreed on PPD. These contradictions are preserved as research findings rather than silently normalized.

### ASUS AirVision M1

ASUS maintains a current product/support surface and sale route for AirVision M1. ASUS describes a transparent wearable display with dual Sony Micro OLED panels, 1920×1080 per eye, USB-C DisplayPort connectivity and host-side multi-screen software. This is a distinct commercial product and not an XREAL/VITURE rebadge in the current evidence.

### Lenovo Legion Glasses generations

Lenovo's original Legion Glasses and Legion Glasses Gen 2 are distinct supported hardware generations. The original support record documents 96 g, 1920×1080, 38° FOV, free-form optics and part number GY21M72722. Gen 2 documents 65 g, 1920×1080, 43.5° FOV, birdbath optics and part number GY21R10236, and Lenovo continues to offer Gen 2 through its US product surface.

The earlier Lenovo Glasses T1, called Lenovo Yoga Glasses in China, is not allocated an additional GLS identity here. Lenovo's 2022 and 2023 material places it in the same direct lineage that became the Legion-branded consumer display. Until evidence establishes a materially different sold hardware platform, T1/Yoga is retained as predecessor/rebrand naming rather than used to inflate the canonical count.

### HUAWEI Vision Glass

Huawei maintains first-party product, specification and support pages for Vision Glass. The specification record documents dual 0.49-inch Micro OLED displays, 2×1920×1080, 60 Hz, birdbath optics, approximately 41° FOV and 112 g including cable. Historical sale evidence establishes that the product crossed the purchase threshold in China. It is therefore a canonical purchaser-history model even if current broad retail availability is no longer established.

### StarV View

StarV View had already been investigated during the MYVU/StarV lineage audit and correctly separated from StarV Air/Air2: View is the heavier birdbath/cinema-display branch, not the XGA010C waveguide lineage. The current Meizu store now supplies the missing acquisition evidence directly, with an active StarV View purchase surface. Canonical admission therefore resolves an orphaned research record rather than creating a newly discovered lineage.

### OPPO Air Glass

OPPO's original Air Glass crossed the purchase threshold: OPPO announced a 4,999-yuan price, preorder availability and March 3, 2022 online/offline sale. It is a detachable monocular assisted-reality display using Micro LED projection and diffraction-waveguide optics. Its successor names do not receive canonical rows here: OPPO explicitly described Air Glass 2 as a concept product not commercially available, and Air Glass 3 as a prototype.

### TCL NXTWEAR AIR

TCL documents NXTWEAR AIR as the second generation after NXTWEAR G, with dual 1080p Micro OLED displays, 47° FOV, 60 Hz, 75 g weight, USB-C/HDMI connectivity and no built-in battery. Historical commercial listings establish that units were actually sold. It sits between NXTWEAR G and NXTWEAR S in the TCL/RayNeo display lineage and should not be collapsed into NXTWEAR G or S. The exact relationship between China-market RayNeo/Thunderbird naming and the existing GLS-0087 RayNeo Air record remains a lineage question, not grounds to suppress the independently sold NXTWEAR AIR identity.

## Explicit non-admissions

- **OPPO Air Glass 2** — concept product; OPPO says it was not commercialized.
- **OPPO Air Glass 3** — prototype at MWC 2024; no purchaser-history row.
- **Lenovo Glasses T1 / Yoga Glasses** — predecessor/rebrand naming retained under the first Legion display lineage pending evidence for materially distinct sold hardware.
- **HUAWEI Vision Glass 2** — no sufficiently authoritative commercial identity located in this audit; do not create a row from secondary naming alone.
- **Existing XREAL, VITURE, RayNeo Air, Rokid Air/Max and TCL NXTWEAR G/G+/S/S+ rows** — collision-checked and intentionally not duplicated.

## Evidence boundary

These admissions establish product identity and acquisition history. They do not promote manufacturer brightness, FOV, PPD, resolution, weight, “4K,” optical-technology, battery-life, comfort, 3DoF, openness, owner-control, hackability or compatibility claims to hands-on fact.

CORNMI in particular receives an explicit conflict flag. The A8 Lite “4K” and “Micro-LED” marketing language should not be normalized into native-display claims while first-party specification tables identify a different resolution and Micro OLED. Physical NDI would be the preferred way to settle weight, panel, EDID/input modes and optical architecture, but the model does not require purchase merely to exist in the catalog.

## Catalog propagation

The highest allocated canonical identifier before this packet is `GLS-0170`. This packet allocates `GLS-0171` through `GLS-0181`. The canonical synchronizer should therefore add eleven rows and advance the active purchaser-history count from 169 to **180**.
