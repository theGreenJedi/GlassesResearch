# AREX ONE

**Canonical ID:** ADJ-0018  
**Category:** Specialized head-worn computing / AR dive mask  
**State:** preorder / crowdfunding; broad delivery not established  
**Evidence state:** functional engineering prototype publicly demonstrated; production specifications and safety claims remain manufacturer-reported unless otherwise noted  
**GlassesResearch bench status:** not tested  
**Hardware lineage:** unresolved; Arexvision states that AREX hardware is developed with a hardware technology partner but does not publicly identify that partner in the source set reviewed on 2026-09-29.

## Why it belongs

AREX ONE is not an eyeglass-frame smart-glasses product, so it does not receive a `GLS-` identifier and does not inflate the smart-glasses count. It is nevertheless directly within GlassesResearch's broader wearable-HCI scope: a head-worn transparent display combines environmental sensing, dive computation and visual augmentation in the user's normal task loop.

## What is established

Public prototype evidence establishes that AREX ONE exists beyond renders or a concept announcement. A functional engineering prototype has been publicly exhibited, and the 2026 James Dyson Award project page documents prototype development and underwater testing. The current crowdfunding campaign provides a documented acquisition route, which clears the adjacent-wearable admission threshold while delivery remains unproven.

The proposed production architecture combines a transparent optical waveguide HUD with a monochrome green MicroLED display and an onboard dive computer. AREX describes standalone underwater operation after configuration, without requiring a phone or network connection for core dive-computer functions.

## Claims that remain claims

Published figures including 640×480 display resolution, approximately 30-degree field of view, peak brightness, transparency, production depth rating, battery endurance and other performance specifications are manufacturer/campaign claims unless independently reproduced.

AREX also describes an optional Marine ID module with a camera and on-device species recognition. That architecture is worth tracking, but GlassesResearch has not verified its production behavior, model performance, privacy behavior or independence from external services.

## Safety and certification boundary

This product can present information relevant to decompression and breathing-gas management. The evidence standard therefore needs to be stricter than for ordinary consumer eyewear.

As of the 2026-09-29 review, AREX materials describe EN 250 / EN 13319 certification as pending. GlassesResearch must not translate a manufacturer depth specification or prototype test into an independently verified safe operating depth. Certification status should be rechecked before shipment and after any material hardware revision.

## Owner control / cloud independence

Core dive-computer operation is claimed to function underwater without a phone or network connection. That is a meaningful cloud-independence signal, but firmware update behavior, account requirements, data export, companion-app dependence, API/SDK access, bootloader access and long-term service survivability remain unresolved.

No open SDK, open firmware or user-serviceable battery path has been established by the evidence reviewed here. Unknown stays unknown.

## Lineage questions

Arexvision publicly describes itself as leading the software ecosystem while working with a hardware technology partner on AREX smart-diving hardware. The partner identity is unresolved. Until evidence establishes the upstream hardware manufacturer or a shared platform, AREX ONE remains its own canonical adjacent-wearable identity and no OEM lineage is inferred.

## Research priorities

1. Identify the unnamed hardware technology partner and any upstream optical/display platform.
2. Track EN 250 / EN 13319 certification to completion or failure.
3. Confirm production hardware specifications independently after shipment.
4. Map firmware updates, companion application, account requirements and data export.
5. Determine whether the Marine ID module performs inference fully on-device in shipping hardware.
6. Establish battery-service policy and realistic lifecycle.
7. Acquire a production unit for bench evaluation if practical.
