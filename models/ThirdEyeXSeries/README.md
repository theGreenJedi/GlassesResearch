# ThirdEye X-Series

ThirdEye's enterprise mixed-reality eyewear lineage is best understood as a family rather than a single product. Surviving first-party material preserves an earlier **X1 Smart Glasses** generation, a well-documented **X2 MR Glasses** generation, and a later **Alpha1 MR Glasses** device. The evidence quality is uneven, so the repository does not inherit X2 specifications backward to X1 or forward to Alpha1.

## X2 MR Glasses

The X2 is the lineage member with the strongest surviving public record and a current acquisition route. ThirdEye lists it at **$2,450** with direct Add To Cart purchasing. It is a standalone enterprise MR headset built around Qualcomm Snapdragon XR1 compute, 4 GB RAM and 64 GB storage, with 1280 × 720 waveguide displays, multiple cameras/depth sensors, GPS, USB-C expansion and a built-in low-resolution thermal sensor.

ThirdEye pairs the hardware with a substantial software stack: RealEye AI, remote collaboration, mobile-device management, workflow tools, VisionEye SLAM development support and Android-based customization. That makes X2 meaningfully more programmable and owner-controllable than appliance-style smart glasses, while still remaining a proprietary enterprise platform rather than an open hardware/firmware system.

Wearability is the obvious compromise. ThirdEye describes the X2 as roughly 11 ounces, and its own guidance emphasizes a head strap and optional hard-hat mounting. Prescription frames are available, but this is industrial head-worn computing rather than discreet everyday eyewear.

The X2's strongest report-card areas are **Hardware (8.0), Software (8.0), Display/HUD (8.0), and Cloud Independence (7.5)**. It is expensive at $2,450, but the price reflects a complete standalone MR computer with enterprise sensors, developer support and a mature deployment stack.

## X1 Smart Glasses

ThirdEye's current enterprise material still references X1 Smart Glasses, establishing X1 as an earlier lineage member. The surviving first-party pages in this pass do not provide a complete X1-specific product sheet or purchase record, so X1 is preserved without inherited X2 scores.

## Alpha1 MR Glasses

ThirdEye's current TAA compliance statement explicitly identifies Alpha1 MR Glasses as a ThirdEye product designed in the United States and assembled in the United States and Taiwan. That is enough to preserve Alpha1 as a real hardware generation, but not enough to assign X2 hardware, pricing or scores to it. A future archival pass should recover Alpha1's original product/store documentation.

## Adjacent ThirdEye devices

ThirdEye's **MIDAS** system places a monocular AR display inside protective masks. It belongs in the site's adjacent head-worn/protective-equipment catalog, not in the smart-glasses count. This is exactly the kind of boundary the wearable-HCI taxonomy is intended to preserve: related augmentation technology, different physical shelf.
