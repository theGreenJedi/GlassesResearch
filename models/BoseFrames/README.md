# Bose Frames family

**GLS-0013 through GLS-0017** cover Bose's audio-eyewear line: Alto, Rondo, Tempo, Tenor, and Soprano.

Bose Frames are open-ear audio glasses rather than visual-display glasses. The first-generation Alto and Rondo use Bluetooth audio, an integrated microphone, a 9-axis head-motion sensor, and Bose Connect. Bose documented both at about 45 g with up to 3.5 hours of music playback and up to 12 hours standby. They do not use Wi-Fi. Bose sold Alto and Rondo from 2019 into the early 2020s and now treats them as legacy products.

The 2020 generation split into a sport model and two style models. **Tempo** uses two 22 mm open-ear drivers, a dual beam-forming microphone array, Bluetooth 5.1, IPX4 water resistance, USB-C charging, and an advertised battery life of up to 8 hours. **Tenor** and **Soprano** use smaller 16 mm speakers, the same dual-beam-forming microphone concept, and an advertised 5.5-hour battery life; Tenor's surviving product documentation specifies Bluetooth 5.1 and IPX2 resistance. These models use the Bose Music/Bose app family.

## Developer and platform notes

Bose Frames were designed primarily as Bluetooth audio accessories with voice-assistant access from the paired phone. Bose's original marketing also tied Alto and Rondo to the now-discontinued Bose AR audio-augmented-reality platform, using head-motion sensing plus phone location/orientation rather than an optical display. Current public Bose support focuses on product operation, app support, compliance, and legacy-device assistance rather than an active Frames SDK ecosystem.
