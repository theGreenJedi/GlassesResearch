# Pivothead Camera Eyewear — lineage profile

Pivothead belongs in the historical camera-glasses branch rather than the display-glasses branch. Its first commercial generation packaged a centered point-of-view camera directly into sunglass frames, with local 1080p recording, still capture, internal storage, microphone, USB transfer, and desktop/mobile configuration. Surviving commercial records show the platform was sold in multiple frame styles, but those style names should not be inflated into separate hardware generations without evidence of materially different electronics.

The first generation is notable less for augmented-reality capability than for **owner-controlled wearable capture**. Core recording was local, files were locally retrievable, and the device did not depend on a vendor cloud to perform its primary job. That gives it unusually strong Cloud Independence for a consumer wearable of its era, even though its proprietary embedded platform limits Openness and Hackability.

Pivothead SMART / Architect Edition represents the second meaningful generation. It preserved the camera-first eyewear form while adding touch/swipe interaction, modular accessories, wireless/live-streaming capability, prescription-compatible lens options, and an explicit developer/SDK story. The surviving evidence clearly establishes a distinct commercial product, but not enough of the generation-specific internal hardware stack has been recovered to justify copying first-generation scores into SMART. Those dimensions remain intentionally ungraded until archived manuals and SDK documentation are recovered.

Neither generation has a near-eye display, so Display/HUD is N/A rather than zero. Likewise, camera features do not by themselves constitute a modern Visual AI system; Visual AI remains N/A unless evidence demonstrates an actual visual-assistant capability.
