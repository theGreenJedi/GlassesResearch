# Optinvent ORA Lineage Profiles

These profiles are derived from the completed lineage research packet in `docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_OPTINVENT_ORA.md` and preserve the distinction between hardware generations, software bundles, prototypes, and adjacent non-glasses products.

## Optinvent ORA-1

Optinvent's ORA-1 was an early standalone Android smart-glasses developer platform rather than a phone-dependent notification accessory. It could run Android applications natively, was promoted with developer tooling including Wikitude integration, and was actually built and delivered through Optinvent's Kickstarter/developer route. Its surviving evidence shows a relatively open application-development model for its era, but not open hardware: owners could build and run their own software without depending on a vendor assistant or cloud, while firmware, schematics and lower-level internals remained proprietary. The device's bulky developer-eyewear form factor limits wearability, and incomplete surviving first-party specifications mean its hardware score remains deliberately ungraded pending archival recovery.

## Optinvent ORA-2

ORA-2 matured the concept into a professional standalone AR computer. Optinvent documents Android AOSP 4.4.2, a dual-core processor/GPU, 5 MP autofocus camera, microphone and audio, 9-axis sensing, GPS, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth LE, trackpad input, a 1200 mAh battery and an approximately 90 g safety-eyewear form factor. Its adjustable Flip-Vu transparent display could place the virtual image directly in or below the wearer's view, and Optinvent shipped the platform with an Android SDK/manual for application development. That combination gives ORA-2 strong owner control and cloud independence by catalog standards: it can run owner-written local Android applications without being confined to a fixed vendor assistant. Its compromises are equally clear—heavy professional styling, aging compute and proprietary firmware/hardware keep it well below the modern openness and wearability benchmarks.

## ORA-X

ORA-X belongs in Optinvent's broader ORA lineage because it reuses the company's transparent-display ideas and Android developer model, but it should not be silently counted as smart glasses. Optinvent explicitly sells it as over-ear smart AR headphones with a flip-down display. The company lists a 499 EUR ex-VAT pre-order route and developer kits, so it is a real acquisition-stage wearable product; the catalog boundary, not lack of commercial intent, is why it remains outside the glasses ledger.

## ORA-EYE and ORA-H

ORA-EYE is a remote-assistance software/service package that operates on ORA-2 hardware, not a separate glasses generation. ORA-H is documented by Optinvent as an early smart-helmet prototype. Both are preserved as lineage context without being promoted into separate smart-glasses model rows.
