# DAQRI Professional AR — Evidence-Derived Profiles

## DAQRI Smart Glasses

DAQRI Smart Glasses were a commercial enterprise augmented-reality system that began worldwide customer shipments in November 2017 at a direct-purchase price of $4,995. They paired a transparent stereoscopic 44-degree display with Intel Core m7-class compute, Intel RealSense depth sensing, wide-angle tracking, an HD color camera, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth connectivity, and DAQRI's spatial-mapping software stack. Their strongest qualities were professional-grade sensing, local spatial tracking, and a comparatively substantial enterprise software/developer platform. Their weaknesses were equally characteristic of first-generation professional AR: conspicuous headset-like wearability, high acquisition cost, proprietary platform dependencies, and a vendor ecosystem that later disappeared. On the common ruler they score strongly in Hardware, Software, Display/HUD, Owner Control and Cloud Independence, but only moderately in Openness and Hackability and poorly in Value relative to their contemporaneous price.

## DAQRI Smart Helmet

DAQRI Smart Helmet belongs beside the Smart Glasses in the DAQRI professional-AR lineage but not in the smart-glasses count. It was an industrial AR helmet with integrated eye protection, audio, see-through display, Intel Core m7 compute, RealSense depth sensing, wide-angle tracking, RGB imaging and thermal imaging. DAQRI explicitly positioned it as a ready-to-deploy development/work platform with APIs and developer tools. The helmet's sensor package and safety-oriented industrial integration make it arguably the more technically ambitious DAQRI wearable, but its helmet form factor, size and weight make ordinary-eyewear wearability inapplicable as a design goal and very poor on the shared Wearability ruler. It therefore belongs on the adjacent **industrial head-worn AR** shelf while retaining the same evidence standards and shared report-card dimensions.

## DAQRI Smart HUD

DAQRI Smart HUD was part of the same broader Visual Operating System ecosystem but was not wearable eyewear. It should be preserved as an adjacent DAQRI display-platform record rather than counted as either glasses or a helmet. If GlassesResearch later opens a vehicle/ambient-interface shelf, Smart HUD belongs there.

## Family significance

DAQRI is a useful historical example of why GlassesResearch now separates form factor from the broader wearable-HCI mission. A single vendor pursued the same augmentation philosophy across a hard-hat wearable, smart glasses and a vehicle HUD. Treating those as one undifferentiated model category would destroy useful distinctions; treating them as unrelated would lose the architectural lineage. The correct representation is one research family with separate form-factor shelves.
