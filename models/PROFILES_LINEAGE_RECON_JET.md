# Evidence-Derived Profiles — Recon Jet Lineage

These profiles are derived from the completed lineage investigation in `docs/report-cards/LINEAGE_RECON_JET.md`. They preserve generation-specific uncertainty rather than inheriting undocumented specifications from the original Jet.

## Recon Jet

Recon Jet was one of the more ambitious sport-focused wearable computers of the mid-2010s: a self-contained Android-based monocular HUD with camera, GPS/activity sensing, ANT+ support, smartphone connectivity and a public SDK rather than a closed appliance-only experience. Recon sold it directly, through specialty retail and through Amazon in the United States, launching at $699 before a documented reduction to $499. Its developer model is the reason it remains technically interesting: third parties could build applications around the wearer’s activity data and display rather than merely consume vendor-selected functions. The long-term ownership story is much weaker. Recon Engage services were later shut down after Intel discontinued the product family, and account/service dependence became a concrete survivability failure. Jet therefore scores well for owner control, openness and hackability relative to its era, but poorly for cloud independence on a present-day ownership ruler.

## Recon Jet Pro

Recon Jet Pro was a later commercial/enterprise member of the Jet family, not merely an announced concept. Intel later identified it explicitly as a Recon product, and teardown evidence documents a US-purchased RI-JET unit at about 85 g with a roughly $992 recorded retail price. What is currently missing is equally important: the available primary trail does not yet cleanly establish which processor, optical, camera, battery, ruggedization or SDK changes distinguished Pro from the original Jet. GlassesResearch therefore admits the product but does not copy Jet’s report-card scores forward. The remaining fields stay ungraded until archived Recon or Intel product sheets can support generation-specific claims.

## Recon Jet Pro+

Recon Jet Pro+ is likewise confirmed by Intel’s own support record as a distinct customer product in the discontinued Recon family. The surviving primary evidence is presently strong enough to prove that the model existed in the market but too thin to justify inherited hardware or software specifications. Its profile is therefore intentionally sparse: Pro+ belongs in the lineage and purchasing history, while its detailed report card remains an archival-recovery problem. That distinction is preferable to turning a model name into a false specification sheet.
