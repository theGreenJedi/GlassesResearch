# Smart-Glasses Model Profiles — display-glasses gap audit

These are desk-research profiles for the 2026-09-04 display-glasses reconciliation. Canonical admission establishes purchaser history and product identity; it does not turn vendor specifications into hands-on measurements or assign report-card scores.

## GLS-0171 — CORNMI NeoVista A8 Lite

**NeoVista A8 Lite** is a host-driven personal-display product sold directly by CORNMI and also surfaced through OEM/marketplace channels. CORNMI currently markets USB-C display connectivity, a wide virtual-screen experience, diopter adjustment and a 90 Hz display, but its own evidence is internally contradictory: storefront/editorial material describes a roughly 70–79 g Micro OLED product while a manufacturer specification page lists 238 g, and the product headline says “Micro-LED” even though the same page's specification table says Micro OLED. GlassesResearch therefore records A8 Lite as a real purchasable identity while leaving weight, panel technology, native timing and “4K” claims unresolved pending stronger evidence or NDI. ## GLS-0172 — CORNMI NeoVista X7 Lite

**NeoVista X7 Lite** is CORNMI's lower-resolution X7-family personal display. The direct store identifies a separate X7 Lite SKU and sells it as a 62°-class, 1920×1080-per-eye Micro OLED display. CORNMI's own pages disagree on details including weight and PPD, so those values remain manufacturer claims rather than verified specifications. The important identity fact is stronger: X7 Lite is separately named, separately sold and materially differentiated from X7 and X7 Pro. ## GLS-0173 — CORNMI NeoVista X7

**NeoVista X7** is the higher-resolution host-driven member of CORNMI's X7 family. CORNMI's direct store currently lists a distinct X7 SKU, 2560×1440-per-eye Micro OLED panels, USB-C DisplayPort input and a 62° claimed FOV. It remains fundamentally a wearable external display: host choice supplies the computing and content, which should yield strong practical cloud independence even though firmware openness and repairability remain unverified. No such ownership score is promoted until the normal report-card evidence path is completed. [Canonical record](/models/THE_LIST/) · [reconciliation](/models/THE_LIST_RECONCILIATION_2026-09-04_DISPLAY_GLASSES/)

## GLS-0174 — CORNMI NeoVista X7 Pro

**NeoVista X7 Pro** is the materially distinct top X7 configuration because CORNMI sells it with built-in Android OS rather than only as a passive host display. CORNMI also lists 2560×1440-per-eye Sony Micro OLED panels, USB-C DisplayPort, stereo speakers, dual microphones and optional 3DoF accessories. The added onboard software/computing layer makes Pro a different ownership and service-dependence problem from X7/X7 Lite, so it receives its own canonical identity. The exact Android build, app surface, update path, storage, root/boot state and offline behavior remain research targets. ## GLS-0175 — ASUS AirVision M1

**ASUS AirVision M1** is a current transparent wearable display aimed at productivity, gaming and media. ASUS documents dual Sony Micro OLED panels at 1920×1080 per eye, USB-C DisplayPort compatibility, built-in audio, gesture controls and AirVision host software for multi-screen layouts. ASUS currently markets an approximately 87 g device and a 100-inch-class virtual display. The architecture is primarily host-driven rather than autonomous, making it relevant to the same portable-monitor lineage as XREAL, VITURE, Rokid and RayNeo without implying common OEM identity. ## GLS-0176 — Lenovo Legion Glasses

**Legion Glasses** is Lenovo's first commercial Legion-branded wearable display generation. Lenovo support documents 1920×1080 resolution, 38° FOV, free-form optics, USB-C DisplayPort 1.2, IMU/gyro sensing, 96 g weight and part number GY21M72722. Lenovo's earlier Glasses T1 / Yoga Glasses material is treated as predecessor/rebrand lineage evidence rather than a separate canonical generation until materially distinct sold hardware is established. The original Legion Glasses is now treated as legacy beside Gen 2. [Canonical record](/models/THE_LIST/) · [reconciliation](/models/THE_LIST_RECONCILIATION_2026-09-04_DISPLAY_GLASSES/)

## GLS-0177 — Lenovo Legion Glasses Gen 2

**Legion Glasses Gen 2** is a distinct second hardware generation and remains part of Lenovo's current Legion ecosystem. Lenovo documents 65 g weight, 1920×1080 resolution, 43.5° FOV, birdbath optics, IMU/gyro sensing, USB-C DisplayPort 1.2 and part number GY21R10236. Lenovo launched the generation in 2025 and later added a software 2D-to-3D mode. The large reduction in weight and optical redesign distinguish it cleanly from the first Legion Glasses. ## GLS-0178 — HUAWEI Vision Glass

**HUAWEI Vision Glass** is Huawei's wired cinema/display-glasses product, sold in China and still supported through first-party pages. Huawei documents dual 0.49-inch Micro OLED displays, 2×1920×1080 resolution, 60 Hz refresh, birdbath optics, about 41° FOV, 53 PPD, 480-nit eye brightness and 112 g weight including cable, plus 0–500-degree myopia adjustment and open-ear stereo. It is host-dependent rather than a standalone AR computer. Its historical commercial route is sufficient for canonical inclusion even though current broad retail status is unclear. ## GLS-0179 — StarV View

**StarV View** is DreamSmart/Meizu's cinema-display branch and is explicitly separate from the StarV Air/MYVU waveguide lineage. First-party specifications identify a 74 g design with Sony OLED, 1920×1080 per eye, 120 Hz, 43.5° FOV, 51 PPD, 3DoF, Type-C input and 0–6.00D adjustment. The Meizu store currently offers StarV View for direct purchase in China. This admission resolves an existing orphaned investigation rather than changing the prior lineage conclusion: View is birdbath-style personal display hardware, not XGA010C or a StarV Air/Air2 optical sibling. ## GLS-0180 — OPPO Air Glass

**OPPO Air Glass** is the original commercially released member of OPPO's assisted-reality glasses program. OPPO priced it at 4,999 yuan and announced March 3, 2022 online/offline sale in China. The hardware is a detachable monocular display using OPPO's Spark Micro Projector, Micro LED and diffraction-waveguide optics, paired with OPPO phones/watch for functions such as notifications, navigation, teleprompter and translation. This is materially different from the later Air Glass 2 and 3 concepts: OPPO explicitly says Air Glass 2 was not commercialized and calls Air Glass 3 a prototype, so those successors do not receive purchaser-history rows. [Canonical record](/models/THE_LIST/) · [reconciliation](/models/THE_LIST_RECONCILIATION_2026-09-04_DISPLAY_GLASSES/)

## GLS-0181 — TCL / RayNeo NXTWEAR AIR

**NXTWEAR AIR** is TCL's documented second-generation wearable display after NXTWEAR G. TCL specifies dual 1920×1080 Micro OLED displays, 60 Hz refresh, 47° FOV, 47 PPD, 75 g weight, stereo speakers, USB-C/HDMI connectivity, no built-in battery and 3DoF sensing. Commercial evidence shows the model was sold, including under TCL/RayNeo branding. It therefore fills a real historical gap between NXTWEAR G and the later NXTWEAR S branch. Its relationship to China-market Thunderbird/RayNeo naming deserves continued lineage work, but that uncertainty does not erase the independently marketed and sold NXTWEAR AIR identity.
