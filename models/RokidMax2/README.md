# Rokid Max 2 — GLS-0094

Rokid Max 2 is the current glasses hardware at the center of Rokid's host-driven AR/display branch.

**Technology lineage:** [Rokid](../../lineages/ROKID.md)  
**Canonical ID:** `GLS-0094`  
**Architecture:** tethered binocular personal display; source-device compute by default

## Identity boundary

Max 2 is the eyewear model. **Rokid AR Spatial** and **Rokid AR Joy 2** are commercial systems built around Max 2 plus different Rokid Station generations; they should not be counted as additional Max 2 eyewear identities.

## Current first-party description

Rokid's AR-series comparison currently describes standalone Max 2 as:

- a lightweight private display for phones, laptops, and consoles;
- direct display from a connected device;
- USB-C DisplayPort connectivity for compatible sources;
- 1920 × 1080 default display operation, with up to 1920 × 1200 from compatible sources;
- 50° field of view;
- refresh rate up to 120 Hz;
- brightness up to 600 nits;
- built-in 0.00D to -6.00D myopia adjustment;
- source-device powered operation.

Rokid separately states that Max 2 requires Station 2 for its fuller spatial-computing experience.

## Storefront contradiction warning

The standalone Max 2 product page currently contains blocks advertising **8 GB RAM, 128 GB storage, Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.2, a 5000 mAh battery, 18 W charging, and YodaOS-Master**. Those values match Station 2-style compute claims and conflict with Rokid's own AR-series comparison describing standalone Max 2 as using a connected phone, computer, or console.

GlassesResearch therefore treats those storefront blocks as a **source contradiction requiring resolution**, not as verified Max 2 onboard-compute specifications.

## Investigation queue

1. Re-establish Max 2 optical/display specifications from model-specific manuals, regulatory filings, and hands-on measurement.
2. Confirm USB-C DisplayPort behavior, EDID modes, refresh rates, 3D modes, power draw, and audio/microphone transport.
3. Separate glasses firmware from Station/Station 2 software and update paths.
4. Test generic host interoperability with Android, Windows, macOS, Linux, consoles, and adapters.
5. Characterize myopia adjustment, IPD behavior, prescription insert compatibility, and optical serviceability.
6. Document operation with no Rokid account, no Rokid software, and no network.
7. Measure serviceability, cable/connector failure modes, and long-term usefulness as a standards-based display peripheral.
