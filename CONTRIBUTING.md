# Contributing to GlassesResearch

Thank you for helping document and preserve the smart-glasses ecosystem.

The full contribution guide is published at [`docs/CONTRIBUTE.md`](docs/CONTRIBUTE.md) and on the public website under **Want to contribute?**

## Quick start

You can contribute by:

- opening an issue with a correction, source, model, question, preservation lead, or research finding;
- submitting a pull request with a focused documentation, evidence, or research improvement;
- sharing hands-on observations with dates, versions, photographs, and reproduction steps;
- identifying manuals, SDKs, software, firmware history, regulatory filings, teardowns, or community projects;
- improving citations, cross-links, evidence labels, accessibility, or navigation.

Small contributions are welcome. A corrected model name, restored link, or properly sourced sentence can prevent future researchers from repeating old work.

## Community Research, corrections, and technical findings

Community researchers, maintainers, reverse engineers, device owners, and project authors are explicitly welcome here.

Good contributions include:

- correcting how GlassesResearch describes your project or technical finding;
- supplying a stronger source, missing context, or chronology correction;
- documenting a protocol, command sequence, service, characteristic, packet, device state, or reproducible behavior;
- reporting a failed reproduction or negative result with the conditions under which it occurred;
- identifying alternative clients, interoperability paths, account/app/cloud dependencies, or owner-controlled workflows;
- linking a community project to the correct hardware model or lineage;
- clarifying what has been directly observed, reproduced, inferred, contradicted, or not yet tested.

You do **not** need to convert your contribution into GlassesResearch's internal format before submitting it. Open an issue or PR with the clearest evidence you have; maintainers can help place and label it correctly.

### Evidence status is preserved

A contribution may be valuable before GlassesResearch independently reproduces it. Please distinguish clearly among:

- directly observed or measured findings;
- reproduced or demonstrated findings;
- primary-source documentation;
- community reports not yet independently reproduced;
- manufacturer claims;
- inference or qualification;
- contradicted findings;
- unproven or not-tested claims.

Merging a contribution does **not** automatically promote a community or manufacturer claim into GlassesResearch **Verified** status. Verification remains a separate evidence decision.

## Pull requests

A focused pull request is the best route when you already know what should change.

Please include:

1. **What changed** — the correction, finding, or improvement.
2. **Why it matters** — what reader, model, lineage, method, or research question it affects.
3. **Evidence and limitations** — links, artifacts, reproduction conditions, uncertainty, negative results, and anything not yet proven.
4. **Validation** — what you checked, reproduced, built, or tested.
5. **Attribution preference** — real name, public handle, project name, pseudonym, or anonymous if relevant.

If you are correcting GlassesResearch coverage of your own project, say so. That context is useful, but the correction will still be evaluated against the available evidence rather than treated as editorial approval.

## Issues

If you do not want to edit the repository, open an issue instead. The **Research finding or correction** issue template is intended for factual corrections, protocol findings, reproduction notes, project-maintainer feedback, and evidence submissions.

Incomplete leads are welcome when clearly labeled. A maintainer can help turn a useful report into the correct durable record.

## Credit and privacy

Contributors may choose to be credited by real name, public handle, project name, pseudonym, or anonymously. Credit may appear on the relevant page, evidence record, commit, pull request, acknowledgment, or a future contributor hall.

If you are the subject or maintainer of material we cover and prefer a different public attribution, tell us. Factual corrections and attribution preferences are welcome; GlassesResearch retains independent editorial judgment about interpretation and conclusions.

The public site follows a no-tracking default: no advertising, no tracking cookies, no behavioral profiling, and no sale of visitor data. Community-specific greetings use only an explicit `?from=` value in a shared contribution link; the site does not inspect referrers or remember the source.

See [`docs/PRIVACY.md`](docs/PRIVACY.md) for the complete privacy promise.

## Core principles

- Knowledge over marketing.
- Evidence over speculation.
- Openness over lock-in.
- Archive first, organize second.
- Verify continuously and attribute always.
- Preserve knowledge, not attention.

See the [full contribution portal](docs/CONTRIBUTE.md) for evidence standards, outreach guidance, contributor recognition, preservation practices, and community expectations.
